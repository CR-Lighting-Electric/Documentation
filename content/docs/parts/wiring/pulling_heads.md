Pulling heads are mechanical termination devices installed on the end of a cable to enable safe, controlled, and efficient installation through conduits, cable trays, duct banks, or underground pulls.

![](/images/pulling-heads-example.png)

1. Force Distribution: Spread pulling tension evenly across the cable’s cross-section to prevent insulation crushing, conductor deformation, or jacket tearing.
2. Attachment Interface: Provide a secure, standardized connection point for pulling grips, winch cables, or rope systems.
3. Torque Management: Swivel-style heads rotate independently to absorb cable twist during bends, reducing torsional stress and preventing kinking.

Common configurations include:

- Swivel Heads: Rotate freely; ideal for long or multi-bend commercial runs.
- Rigid/Cone Heads: Fixed orientation; used for short, straight pulls or where rotation must be prevented.
- Flat/Plate Heads: Designed for ribbon, fiber, or flat data cables.
- Specialty/Coaxial Heads: Sized for communication, CATV, or instrumentation cables.

Constructed from aluminum, stainless steel, or engineered composites, pulling heads are selected based on cable outer diameter (OD), maximum allowable pull force (per NEC Table 310.15 and manufacturer pull-force charts), environmental exposure, and compatibility with pulling equipment. In commercial projects, proper pulling head specification minimizes installation time, reduces labor costs, prevents costly cable damage, and ensures compliance with NFPA 70 Article 300.17 (Mechanical Execution of Work).

## Naming Convention

`SIZE` `(TYPE)` `(HEAD)` `Pulling-Head`

| Descriptor | Explanation                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `SIZE`     | The diameter of the pulling head in typical wire fashion such as 300 KCMIL, 2 AWG, 1/0 AWG, etc.                                                                                        |
| `(TYPE)`   | The type of connection the pulling head utilizes, with the most common being Crimping (use Crimp-Type). If left off, assume Crimp-Type.                                                 |
| `(HEAD)`   | The type of connection head used to interface with the wiring. Includes Swivel-Head, Rigid-Head, Plate-Head, or some other specialty -Head. If left off, assume the typical Rigid-Head. |

### Example Names

- 500 KCMIL Pulling-Head
- 12 AWG Crimp-Type Pulling-Head
- 4/0 AWG Compression-Type Swivel-Head Pulling-Head

## Units of Measure - Units

Pulling heads are usually purchased in units. However, they are sometimes included in the purchase sale of large wire orders depending on vendor.
