## Overview

An anchor is a fastener or insert that gives a screw or bolt something to hold in a base material that cannot hold it alone: hollow drywall, solid concrete, brick, or block. On electrical orders anchors fasten boxes, strut, conduit straps, panels, cabinets, and equipment to walls and slabs. The anchor type is set by the base material first, so this page covers two families that share a name but little else: drywall anchors for hollow walls, and expansion anchors for concrete and masonry.

![](/images/anchor-example.png)

Scope against neighboring pages: concrete screws (Tapcon type) are on the Screws page, lag bolts and anchor-style bolts are on the Bolts page, and the self-anchoring hangers for threaded rod are on the Threaded Rod Hangers page. This page covers the anchor itself, the insert or expansion device that is set into the wall or slab.

**Field names:** Sheetrock (drywall) anchors are typically called **"Pig Dicks"** on the field. The name is slang and is used for the wallboard anchors in general, so a vendor will not recognize it; confirm the style (plastic expansion, self-drilling threaded, or toggle) before ordering, and write the order with the plain-language name from the convention below. "Sheetrock" is itself a brand name used for drywall in general.

Drywall (hollow wall) anchor types:

- **Plastic expansion anchor** — a plastic sheath that splits and expands as a screw is driven in. Needs a pilot hole slightly smaller than the sheath. Light duty (published ratings around 5 to 20 lb), and not reusable.
- **Self-drilling threaded anchor** — a plastic or metal anchor with wide external threads and a sharp tip that cuts into the wallboard, driven in with a screwdriver with no pilot hole. Metal versions hold more than plastic. Overtightening can crumble the drywall.
- **Hollow-wall anchor (Molly)** — a metal sleeve that expands behind the wall as the screw is tightened. Medium to heavy duty (published ratings around 25 to 75 lb).
- **Toggle bolt** — a machine bolt with a spring-loaded or drop-through toggle that opens flat behind the wall. The strongest of the drywall group (published ratings around 30 to 100 lb or more), but it needs a larger hole and the toggle is lost if the bolt is removed.
- **Strap-toggle anchor** — a steel toggle on plastic ratchet straps, pulled tight against the back of the wall. Medium to heavy duty.

Concrete and masonry anchor types:

- **Wedge anchor** — a threaded stud with a clip at the base that wedges against the hole as the nut is tightened. For solid concrete only. The hole is drilled with a carbide bit of the same diameter as the anchor.
- **Sleeve anchor** — a bolt or stud with an expanding sleeve, used in concrete, brick, and block. The hole is drilled with a bit the same diameter as the anchor.
- **Drop-in anchor** — an internally threaded, flush-set anchor that is set with a setting tool and accepts a bolt or threaded rod, used for hangers and strut where a clean surface is wanted.
- **Lag shield** — a metal shield that holds a lag screw in masonry or concrete.
- **Plastic expansion anchor (masonry)** — a ribbed plastic plug for light loads in block and brick.

Anchors are selected based on:

1. **Base material** — drywall, solid concrete, brick or block, which sets the family.
2. **Type** — the anchor style for the load and for whether the fixture needs to come off and go back.
3. **Size** — the diameter, and for threaded anchors the bolt or screw size it accepts.
4. **Length** — the anchor length, which for expansion anchors is the fixture thickness plus embedment plus nut and washer.
5. **Material and finish** — zinc plated, hot-dip galvanized, or stainless steel for damp or corrosive locations; plastic for light drywall loads.
6. **Load** — the published rating for the type, with the weight shared across the anchors used.

## Naming Convention

When identifying anchors for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
SIZE TYPE BASE (LENGTH-Length) (FINISH) Anchor
```

### Example Names

- `1/2" Wedge Concrete 3-3/4"-Length Zinc-Plated Anchor`
- `3/8" Wedge Concrete 3"-Length Stainless-Steel Anchor`
- `3/8" Sleeve Concrete 2-1/4"-Length Zinc-Plated Anchor`
- `3/8" Drop-In Concrete Zinc-Plated Anchor`
- `1/4" Lag-Shield Masonry 1-1/2"-Length Zinc-Plated Anchor`
- `#8 Self-Drilling Drywall 1-1/4"-Length Zinc-Plated Anchor`
- `#8 Self-Drilling Drywall 1-1/4"-Length Nylon Anchor`
- `#8 Plastic-Expansion Drywall 1-1/2"-Length Nylon Anchor`
- `1/8" Toggle Drywall 3"-Length Zinc-Plated Anchor`
- `#8 Hollow-Wall Drywall 2"-Length Zinc-Plated Anchor`

## Typical Units of Measure

|Unit|Typical Use|
|---|---|
|**Each (EA)**|Occasional use for a single specialty anchor, such as a large wedge anchor.|
|**Pack**|Common retail packaging for drywall and plastic anchors, typically in the **25 to 100** range.|
|**Box**|Standard contractor packaging for wedge, sleeve, and drop-in anchors, commonly **25 to 100 per box** depending on the size.|
|**Case**|Distributor bulk quantity for larger projects.|

### Ordering Help

- Pick the **family by the base material first**: drywall anchors in hollow walls, and wedge, sleeve, or drop-in anchors in concrete and masonry. Wedge anchors are for solid concrete only.
- Use a drywall anchor **only where there is no stud**; where a stud is behind the box or fixture, use a screw into the stud instead.
- Choose the **drywall anchor by the load**: plastic expansion and self-drilling anchors for light loads, hollow-wall and toggle anchors for medium to heavy loads. Check the weight rating on the package and divide the load among the anchors used.
- Do not **overtighten self-drilling anchors**; the wallboard can crumble and the anchor will spin.
- For concrete anchors, **drill with a carbide bit of the size the manufacturer specifies**. For wedge anchors the bit matches the anchor diameter, with extra depth beyond the embedment for dust and cuttings. For example, a 1/2" wedge anchor needs a 1/2" hole and 2-1/4" minimum embedment, and a 5/8" wedge anchor needs 2-3/4".
- Keep **spacing and edge distance**: for wedge anchors, at least 10 anchor diameters between anchors and 5 diameters from an unsupported edge.
- Torque the nut to the manufacturer's specification; for example, published wedge anchor values are 50 to 60 ft-lb for 1/2" and 75 to 90 ft-lb for 5/8". The published numbers are installation references and not design loads, so use the project's engineered design for structural or overhead loads.
- Use **Stainless-Steel or Hot-Dip-Galvanized** in damp, outdoor, or corrosive locations, and keep the anchor compatible with the fixture metal to avoid galvanic corrosion.
- Order the **setting tool for drop-in anchors** and the **carbide bit** with the anchors, since the wrong bit or an unset anchor fails to hold.
