## Overview

A threaded rod hanger is a self-anchoring fastener that screws or drives directly into the overhead structure and presents a female-threaded end that threaded rod screws into. It gives a fast, one-piece way to start a rod drop for conduit trapezes, cable tray, pipe, HVAC equipment, and other suspended loads without a separate anchor and nut. It is the starting point of the hanger runs described on the All-Thread Rod page, and it sits alongside the Rod Couplings page, which joins one rod to another.

![](/images/rod-hanger-example.png)

In the field these are called **"Sammys,"** after the Sammys line made by ITW Buildex, the same way "Minnie" and "Bat Wing" are used elsewhere in this documentation. The name is a brand that has become the generic term, so a request for "Sammys" means a threaded rod hanger of any make.

Threaded rod hangers are grouped by the structure they fasten to:

- **Wood** — self-drilling, self-tapping screws for wood joists, 2x4s, OSB, plywood, and wood beams, installed with no pre-drilling. Available in vertical, horizontal (sidewinder), and swivel mounts.
- **Steel** — self-drilling screws for structural steel such as purlins, beams, and open web joists, installed without a pilot hole. Vertical and horizontal mounts.
- **Metal deck (direct-attach)** — anchors for thin metal deck and thin purlin that need no retaining nut, in vertical, horizontal, and swivel versions; the install calls for a pre-drilled hole.
- **Concrete** — vertical anchors based on a concrete screw, for cast-in-place and precast concrete and open web joists; the install calls for a pre-drilled pilot hole.
- **Drywall and plaster** — a hole-set hanger with clamping wings that spread behind the surface; vertical mount only.

The mount direction sets how the rod comes off the hanger:

- **Vertical** — the rod hangs straight down from the structure, the normal case for a trapeze drop.
- **Horizontal (sidewinder)** — the rod runs sideways off the hanger, used mainly for seismic bracing, where the hanger anchors the end of a brace run.
- **Swivel** — the rod can pivot on the hanger, used where the rod must be angled, as in a seismic brace.

Threaded rod hangers are selected based on:

1. **Substrate** — the structure being fastened to: Wood, Steel, Metal-Deck, Concrete, or Drywall, which sets both the product family and the install method.
2. **Mount** — Vertical for a plain drop, Horizontal or Swivel for seismic bracing or angled rod.
3. **Rod size** — 1/4-20, 3/8-16, or 1/2-13. Most products are made for 3/8-16 rod; the 1/2-13 size is less common and uses a larger nut driver.
4. **Screw length** — the length of the screw that goes into the structure (commonly 1", 2", or 3" on wood hangers). It governs pullout capacity, and NFPA 13 work calls for at least 2" of embedment in wood.
5. **Material** — Zinc-Plated-Steel is the standard finish; Stainless-Steel versions are made for some wood models where corrosion resistance is needed.
6. **Load and listing** — pullout capacity depends on the substrate, the thickness of the member, and the screw length; the manufacturer's tables list ultimate pullout and a lower test load, and many models are UL 203 or UL 203A listed as pipe hangers.

## Naming Convention

When identifying threaded rod hangers for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
ROD-Rod MOUNT SUBSTRATE LENGTH-Length TAPPING MATERIAL Rod-Hanger
```

### Example Names


- `1/4"-20-Rod Vertical Wood 2"-Length Stainless-Steel Rod-Hanger`
- `3/8"-16-Rod Horizontal Steel 1"-Length Zinc-Plated-Steel Rod-Hanger`
- `1/2"-13-Rod Swivel Drywall 3"-Length Self-Tapping Stainless-Steel Rod-Hanger`

### Convention Notes

| Descriptor   | Explanation                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `ROD`        | The threaded rod the hanger receives: 1/4"-20, 3/8"-16, or 1/2"-13.                                                                                                          |
| `MOUNT`      | Vertical (rod hangs straight down), Horizontal (sidewinder, rod runs sideways, used for seismic bracing), or Swivel (rod pivots, used for angled rod and seismic braces).    |
| `SUBSTRATE`  | Wood, Steel, Metal-Deck, Concrete, or Drywall: the structure the hanger fastens to, which sets the product family and the install method.                                    |
| `LENGTH`     | The screw length that goes into the structure (for example 1in, 2in, or 3in); NA for models without a user-selected screw length, such as the direct-attach metal deck type. |
| `(TAPPING)`  | Tapping ability of the rod hanger; include "Self-Tapping" if the rod hanger needs to include a self tapping ability when working with tougher substrates.                    |
| `(MATERIAL)` | Zinc-Plated-Steel (standard) or Stainless-Steel (offered on some wood models for corrosion resistance). Default to Zinc-Plated-Steel.                                        |

### Ordering Help

- Match the hanger to the **substrate and its thickness**; the manufacturer limits the steel family to steel up to 1/2" thick and lists a minimum thickness for the wood sidewinder, so confirm the structure is thick enough before ordering.
- Order the **specified nut driver** along with the hangers. The manufacturer states that using its nut drivers is required for the warranty, and the 1/2" rod size takes a larger driver than the 3/8" size.
- Follow the **install method for the family**: wood and steel hangers are driven without pre-drilling, while the metal deck type (including its swivel and sidewinder seismic versions) and the concrete type need a pilot hole. Concrete hangers must not be driven in impact mode.
- Do not use **ultimate pullout values as working loads**; the catalog lists a lower test load beside the ultimate value, and the project's engineered design load governs. For seismic bracing, confirm the horizontal and swivel models, rod length, and slenderness limits against the engineering documents and NFPA 13 tables rather than defaulting to a vertical hanger.
- Specify **Stainless-Steel** where the wood hanger is in a damp or corrosive location, and confirm the rod, nuts, and washers on the same drop are a compatible material.
- Threaded rod hangers are **a companion to the rod order**: add matching All-Thread Rod, Nuts, Washers, and Rod Couplings for the drop, and the driver tools, to the same order.
