## Overview

A washer is a flat, hole-centered disc placed under a bolt head or nut to distribute clamping load over a wider surface area, protect the substrate from damage, or resist loosening under vibration — a small, easy-to-overlook item that's nonetheless a routine companion purchase alongside bolts, screws, and threaded rod on nearly every electrical hardware order (strut fittings, panel mounting, grounding lugs, equipment mounting, and general fastening throughout the categories already covered in this documentation).

![](/images/washer-example.png)

Washers fall into a few functional families:

- **Flat (plain) washers** — the general-purpose washer, spreading clamping load and protecting the surface under the bolt head/nut; available in two inch-pattern standards per ASME B18.22.1 — **USS** (larger outer diameter, more load distribution) and **SAE** (smaller outer diameter, tighter clearance) — with metric washers following ISO 7089/7090/7092/7093-1 instead.
- **Split lock (helical spring) washers** — a split-ring washer with a helical twist that provides spring tension and an edge bite against the mating surface, resisting loosening under light-to-moderate vibration; suited to non-structural joints rather than heavy structural connections.
- **Toothed lock washers** — internal-tooth or external-tooth washers whose teeth bite into the mating surface for anti-rotation resistance; commonly used in electrical grounding connections (where the tooth bite also helps ensure a clean metal-to-metal bonding contact through paint or light corrosion) and thin-sheet-metal applications.
- **Fender washers** — a flat washer with a significantly larger outer diameter relative to its hole size compared to a standard flat washer; used to spread load over a wider area on thin, soft, or easily-damaged material (sheet metal, wood, fiberglass) where a standard washer's smaller bearing surface could deform or pull through the substrate.

Washers are selected based on:

1. **Type** — Flat for general load distribution and surface protection, Split-Lock or Toothed-Lock where vibration resistance or grounding bite is needed, Fender where extra bearing surface on thin/soft material is needed.
2. **Bolt/screw size fit** — matched to the fastener's diameter (e.g., #8, #10, 1/4", 3/8", 1/2"); the washer's inner hole must clear the fastener while its outer diameter matches the intended pattern (USS/SAE, or a fender washer's oversized OD).
3. **Material** — Zinc-Plated-Steel (standard, indoor/dry corrosion protection), Stainless-Steel (304/A2 for general corrosion resistance, 316/A4 for marine/saline/coastal exposure), Hot-Dip-Galvanized (heavier corrosion protection for exterior use), or Brass (nonferrous, used where galvanic compatibility with brass/bronze hardware matters).
4. **Inch pattern (flat washers specifically)** — USS or SAE, matched to the fastener and application's clearance/load-distribution needs.
5. **Application context** — grounding/bonding connections favor toothed lock washers for their bite through surface coatings; general structural fastening favors flat washers, often paired with a separate lock washer or nut; thin/soft material favors fender washers.

## Naming Convention

When identifying washers for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
SIZE TYPE (MATERIAL) Washer
```

### Example Names

- `1/4" Flat Zinc-Plated-Steel Washer`
- `1/4" Flat Stainless-Steel Washer`
- `3/8" Split-Lock Zinc-Plated-Steel Washer`
- `#10 Toothed-Lock Stainless-Steel Washer`
- `1/4" Fender Washer`
- `1/2" Flat Hot-Dip-Galvanized Washer`
- `1/4" Flat Washer`

### Convention Notes

| Descriptor   | Explanation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `SIZE`       | The fastener diameter the washer is sized for (e.g., #8, #10, 1/4in, 3/8in, 1/2in).                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `TYPE`       | Flat (general-purpose load distribution), Split-Lock (vibration resistance via spring tension), Toothed-Lock (anti-rotation bite, common in grounding connections), or Fender (oversized OD for thin/soft material).                                                         |
| `(MATERIAL)` | Zinc-Plated-Steel (standard indoor/dry), Stainless-Steel (304/A2 general corrosion resistance, or specify 316/A4 for marine/coastal), Hot-Dip-Galvanized (heavier exterior corrosion protection), or Brass (nonferrous/galvanic compatibility). Default to Zinc-Plated-Steel |

### Ordering Help

- For **grounding/bonding connections**, specify a toothed lock washer, not a plain flat or split-lock washer — the tooth bite through paint or light surface corrosion is what helps ensure reliable metal-to-metal contact at the connection.
- Confirm **USS vs. SAE pattern** for flat washers based on the application — USS for wider load distribution on larger clearance holes, SAE where a tighter-fitting, smaller-OD washer is needed.
- Use a **fender washer** wherever the mating surface is thin, soft, or easily deformed (sheet metal, wood, fiberglass enclosures) and a standard flat washer's bearing surface would be too small to prevent pull-through.
- Match **material to environment and galvanic compatibility** — zinc-plated steel for standard indoor use, stainless (304 or 316) for corrosion resistance and coastal/marine exposure, and confirm washer material won't create a galvanic corrosion pairing with dissimilar-metal hardware it will be installed against.
- Washers are a **small, high-volume, easy-to-underorder item** — since they're a companion part to nearly every bolted connection across raceway, enclosure, and equipment hardware, it's worth keeping standard sizes/types in stock rather than ordering them piecemeal per job.
