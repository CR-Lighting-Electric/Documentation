## Overview

A safety harness (full-body harness) is fall-protection PPE worn to arrest a fall, position a worker for hands-free task access, or enable retrieval from a confined space — the harness connects to a lanyard, self-retracting lifeline (SRL), or positioning line, which in turn connects to an anchorage point. A harness alone is not a complete fall-protection system; it must be paired with a compatible connecting device, a suitable rated anchorage point, adequate fall clearance below the work area, and an established rescue plan before use.

The number and location of D-rings (the metal attachment points on the harness) determine what the harness can be used for:

- **Dorsal (back) D-ring** — the primary attachment point, centered between the shoulder blades, required for standard fall-arrest use; OSHA 29 CFR 1910.140 requires dorsal D-ring attachment at shoulder level with anchorage rated for 5,000 lb per user.
- **Sternal (chest) D-ring** — used for ladder-climbing systems, restraint work, or rescue scenarios where free-fall distance must be tightly controlled.
- **Hip/side D-rings** — used for work positioning (holding the worker in place for hands-free task access), not for fall arrest itself; always paired with a separate positioning lanyard as a secondary system alongside dorsal fall-arrest capability.
- **Shoulder D-rings** — used for confined-space retrieval and vertical extraction/rescue operations.

Harness types follow from this D-ring configuration and the task:

- **General fall-arrest harness** — dorsal D-ring only, paired with an energy-absorbing lanyard or SRL; the standard choice for general construction and maintenance work at height.
- **Climbing harness** — dorsal and sternal D-rings plus hip attachment points, for guided ascent/ladder-climbing systems.
- **Positioning harness** — hip D-rings with a positioning lanyard as a secondary system, alongside dorsal fall-arrest capability.
- **Confined-space/rescue harness** — shoulder D-rings for vertical extraction, combined with dorsal D-ring arrest capability.

Safety harnesses are selected based on:

1. **D-ring configuration** — matched to the specific task (fall arrest only, climbing, positioning, or confined-space retrieval); a harness under-equipped for the task (e.g., no hip D-rings for a positioning task) can't be safely adapted after the fact.
2. **Weight capacity** — commonly rated 130-310 lb per ANSI Z359.11-2021, which must account for the worker's body weight plus clothing, tools, and any other worn equipment, not body weight alone.
3. **Size** — sized to the individual worker (leg straps snug with roughly two fingers of clearance, chest strap centered, dorsal D-ring correctly positioned between the shoulder blades); improper fit both reduces comfort and can compromise fall-arrest performance.
4. **Padding/comfort features** — shoulder, leg, and waist padding, and sub-pelvic straps to support the seat area and reduce suspension trauma risk during an extended post-fall suspension before rescue.
5. **Buckle/adjustment style** — tongue-buckle, quick-connect, or mating buckles, affecting how quickly the harness can be donned and adjusted.

## Naming Convention

When identifying safety harnesses for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
SIZE BRAND DRING WEIGHT-CAP Harness
```

### Example Names

- `Large Miller Dorsal-Only 310lb Harness`
- `Medium Miller 3D-Ring 310lb Harness`
- `X-Large 3M-DBI-SALA Dorsal-Sternal-Hip 400lb Harness`
- `Large 3M-DBI-SALA 4D-Ring 310lb Harness`
- `Universal Miller Dorsal-Only 310lb Harness`

### Convention Notes

|Descriptor|Explanation|
|---|---|
|SIZE|Small, Medium, Large, X-Large, or Universal (adjustable single-size), fitted to the individual worker. Leads the name per this category's established SIZE-first convention.|
|BRAND|The manufacturer (e.g., Miller, 3M/DBI-SALA, MSA, Guardian); second field per this category's convention.|
|DRING|Dorsal-Only (standard fall arrest), 3D-Ring or Dorsal-Sternal-Hip (climbing/positioning), 4D-Ring (climbing plus positioning plus shoulder retrieval), or the specific combination needed — matched to the task, not a default.|
|WEIGHT-CAP|The harness's rated capacity in pounds (e.g., 310lb, 400lb) per ANSI Z359.11-2021, accounting for worker weight plus clothing, tools, and worn equipment.|
|Catalog number|Vendor catalogs also carry their own part numbers/model numbers; provide both the plain-language name and the catalog number when known, since exact D-ring placement and buckle style are brand- and model-specific.|

|Unit|Typical Use|

### Ordering Help

- Always match **D-ring configuration to the actual task** — a dorsal-only harness is adequate for general fall-arrest work, but positioning tasks need hip D-rings and confined-space retrieval needs shoulder D-rings; the harness can't be retrofitted with additional D-rings after purchase.
- Confirm **weight capacity accounts for tools and equipment**, not just body weight — a worker's actual loaded weight (body plus tool belt, PPE, and carried materials) is what must stay within the harness's rated capacity, commonly 130-310 lb per ANSI Z359.11-2021.
- A harness is **one component of a complete fall-protection system** — always confirm a compatible lanyard/SRL, a rated anchorage point, adequate fall clearance, and a rescue plan are all in place; ordering a harness alone doesn't complete the PPE requirement.
- **Size per individual worker**, not by bulk default — proper fit (snug leg straps, centered chest strap, correctly positioned dorsal D-ring) affects both comfort and fall-arrest performance, and is checked against the manufacturer's specific fit instructions on the product label.
- Safety harnesses are subject to **periodic inspection and removal-from-service requirements** — track issue dates and inspection schedules per the manufacturer's instructions rather than treating a harness as a one-time purchase with indefinite service life.
