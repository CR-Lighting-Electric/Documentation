## Overview

A self-retracting lifeline (SRL) — called a "yo-yo" on the field — is the fall-arrest connecting device between a worker's safety harness (see the Safety Harnesses page) and an anchorage point. Internally, an SRL houses a spooled cable or web line under light spring tension that pays out and retracts as the worker moves, and engages an internal braking mechanism to stop the line almost immediately if it senses the rapid acceleration of a fall — arresting the fall in a much shorter distance than a standard shock-absorbing lanyard, which matters where fall clearance below the work area is limited.

Lifelines are distinguished along a few key lines:

- **Line material** — Cable (typically 3/16" steel, stainless or galvanized) resists cuts, abrasion, and heat damage, making it the standard choice for harsh environments and leading-edge applications; Webbing (synthetic, e.g., nylon, polyester, or Dyneema) is lighter and, being nonconductive, is the preferred choice around electrical hazards or sensitive equipment where a conductive steel cable would be a liability.
- **Device size/length category:**
    - **Personal SRL (SRL-P)** — compact and lightweight, 6-9 ft working length, mounted directly to the harness's dorsal D-ring rather than to a separate anchor; functions similarly to a self-retracting lanyard.
    - **Standard/block SRL** — larger, heavier, connects to a separate anchorage point, with working lengths commonly 20-60 ft for broader workspace coverage.
    - **Rescue SRL (SRL-R)** — 60 ft and up (to 180 ft on some models), built for vertical retrieval/rescue operations in addition to fall arrest.
- **ANSI Z359.14-2021 class** — the current standard replaced the older Class A/B designation with:
    - **Class 1** — anchorage at or above the dorsal D-ring; standard overhead-anchor applications.
    - **Class 2** — anchorage rated for use up to 5 ft below the D-ring, and specifically rated (via sharp-edge drop testing) for leading-edge work where the fall line could run across an unprotected edge (roofing, steel erection, bridge work); this replaced the older "SRL-LE" naming. Both classes share a maximum arrest force cap of 1,800 lb.

Lifelines are selected based on:

1. **Class** — Class 1 for standard overhead-anchor fall arrest; Class 2 wherever the anchor point may sit below the D-ring or the fall line could run across a leading edge.
2. **Line material** — Cable for harsh/abrasive environments and leading-edge durability; Webbing for lighter weight and nonconductive use around electrical hazards.
3. **Working length** — SRL-P (6-9 ft, direct-to-harness) for compact/close-anchor work; standard SRL (20-60 ft) for broader coverage; SRL-R (60-180 ft) where rescue/retrieval capability is also needed.
4. **Anchorage compatibility** — confirmed against the specific class rating and the actual anchor point's position relative to the D-ring.
5. **Housing material** — thermoplastic polymer housings reduce weight versus older metal housings while maintaining impact resistance; a consideration for worker fatigue on tasks involving extended wear.

## Naming Convention

When identifying lifelines for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
CLASS MATERIAL LENGTH BRAND Lifeline
```

### Example Names

- `Class-1 Cable 30ft 3M-DBI-SALA Lifeline`
- `Class-2 Cable 20ft Miller Lifeline`
- `Class-1 Webbing 9ft MSA Lifeline`
- `Class-2 Webbing 11ft FallTech Lifeline`
- `Class-1 Cable 60ft Guardian Lifeline`

### Convention Notes

|Descriptor|Explanation|
|---|---|
|CLASS|Class-1 (anchorage at/above the D-ring, standard fall arrest) or Class-2 (anchorage up to 5 ft below the D-ring, leading-edge rated) per ANSI Z359.14-2021.|
|MATERIAL|Cable (steel, harsh environments, leading-edge durability) or Webbing (synthetic, lighter, nonconductive for electrical hazard areas).|
|LENGTH|The working length (e.g., 6ft, 9ft for SRL-P; 20ft-60ft standard; 60ft+ for SRL-R rescue models).|
|BRAND|The manufacturer (e.g., 3M/DBI-SALA, Miller, MSA, FallTech, Guardian); confirm anchorage and harness D-ring compatibility against the specific brand/model.|
|Catalog number|Vendor catalogs also carry their own part numbers/model numbers; provide both the plain-language name and the catalog number when known, since exact class rating, arrest force, and housing material are brand- and model-specific.|

|Unit|Typical Use|
|---|---|
|**Each (EA)**|Standard unit; lifelines are issued and tracked individually, often per worker or per anchor station.|

### Ordering Help

- Always confirm **Class rating matches the anchorage position** — a Class 1 device used with an anchor point below the D-ring, or on a leading-edge application, is a misuse of the equipment; Class 2 is required wherever the anchor may sit below the D-ring or the line could run across an unprotected edge.
- Match **line material to the environment** — cable for harsh, abrasive, or leading-edge conditions; webbing for lighter weight and nonconductive safety around electrical hazards, which is especially relevant for this catalog's electrical trade context.
- Choose **working length by task, not by default** — an SRL-P (6-9 ft, direct-to-harness) suits close-anchor work; a standard SRL (20-60 ft) suits broader coverage; an SRL-R is only needed where rescue/retrieval capability is specifically required.
- A lifeline is **one component of a complete fall-protection system** — same as noted on the Safety Harnesses page, confirm compatible harness D-ring, a rated anchorage point, adequate fall clearance, and a rescue plan are all in place.
- Like harnesses, lifelines are subject to **periodic inspection and removal-from-service requirements** — track issue dates and inspection schedules per the manufacturer's instructions, and never substitute a damaged unit (frayed cable, cut webbing, housing cracks) rather than removing it from service.
