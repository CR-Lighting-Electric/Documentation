## Overview

Clothing merchandise covers general apparel purchasing — tops, bottoms, outerwear, footwear, and accessories — for uniform programs, promotional/branded merchandise, workwear, or general office/organizational stock. As with office supplies, clothing is ordered by brand-specific catalog SKU rather than an engineering specification, so the naming convention on this page leads with **size**, then **brand**, since those two fields are the most common source of a wrong-item order.

![](/images/clothing-example.png)

Common subcategories covered under general clothing merchandise include:

1. **Tops** — t-shirts, polos, button-downs, sweatshirts/hoodies.
2. **Bottoms** — pants, shorts, skirts.
3. **Outerwear** — jackets, vests, coats.
4. **Footwear** — shoes, boots, and related work/safety footwear.
5. **Accessories** — hats, belts, gloves, and similar smaller items.

**Sizing systems** are the single biggest source of ordering confusion in this category, and it's worth understanding the two main conventions before placing any order:

- **Alpha sizing** — uses letters such as XS, S, M, and L, and is primarily universal, most commonly applied to knit clothing such as t-shirts, sweatshirts, hoodies, and leggings, where the alpha sizing chart can cover a broader range of body sizes The standard grading rule for alpha sizing is roughly 2 inches of circumference between sizes, meaning a single letter size covers a wider range of actual body measurements than a numeric size does.
- **Numeric sizing** — commonly used for clothing made of woven fabrics in fit-essential styles such as dress pants, shirts, structured jackets, and fitted dresses, with a standard grading rule of roughly 1 inch of circumference between sizes, offering more precise fit at the cost of more distinct sizes to stock.
- **Men's pants** are typically presented in a Waist x Inseam format (e.g., 32x32, 34x30) rather than a single alpha or numeric size.
- **Sizing is brand-specific and not perfectly interchangeable** — even numerical sizing, which appears straightforward, can vary meaningfully between brands, and descriptive/alpha sizes are especially prone to this, since what counts as a "Small" in one brand can be a "Medium" or even "Large" in another. Never assume a size carries over exactly between brands, even within the same garment type.

**Fit/demographic category** is a required field alongside size, since the same size label (e.g., "M" or "32") means different things across Men's, Women's, and Youth/Kids' lines, and garments are cut differently across these categories even at a nominally identical size label.

## Naming Convention

When identifying clothing merchandise for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
SIZE (BRAND) FIT COLOR MATERIAL ITEM
```

### Example Names

- `Medium Hanes Unisex Navy Cotton T-Shirt`
- `32x32 Carhartt Mens Khaki Cotton-Duck Work-Pant`
- `Large Nike Womens White Polyester Polo`
- `10 Timberland Mens Brown Leather Work-Boot`
- `XL Carhartt Unisex Black Fleece Hoodie
- `One-Size Carhartt Unisex Gray Acrylic Beanie`

### Convention Notes

| Descriptor | Explanation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `SIZE`     | The size label, in whichever convention applies to the garment: Alpha (XS–XXL), Numeric (e.g., 8, 32), Waist-x-Inseam (e.g., 32x32) for pants, or a shoe size (e.g., 10). Leads the name, since size mismatch is the most common wrong-item order in this category, and sizing is not interchangeable across brands even at an identical label. |
| `(BRAND)`  | The manufacturer/brand name, hyphenated if multi-word. Always second, since brand affects both fit and sizing scale even when the size label matches.                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| `FIT`      | Mens, Womens, Unisex, or Youth. The same size label means a different actual fit across these categories — never assume interchangeability.                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `TYPE`     | The garment category (T-Shirt, Polo, Work-Pant, Hoodie, Work-Boot, Beanie, etc.).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| `COLOR`    | The garment color as named by the manufacturer.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `MATERIAL` | The primary fabric or construction material (Cotton, Polyester, Fleece, Cotton-Duck, Leather, Acrylic), relevant to durability, care requirements, and climate suitability.                                                                                                                                                                     |

## Typical Units of Measure

|Unit|Typical Use|
|---|---|
|**Each (EA)**|Standard unit for footwear, outerwear, and individually fitted items.|
|**Pair**|Standard unit for footwear and other two-piece items (gloves).|
|**Pack**|Common packaging for basics ordered in bulk (t-shirts, socks), typically **3, 6, or 12 per pack**.|
|**Case**|Distributor bulk quantity for large uniform or promotional-merchandise orders, made up of multiple packs.|

### Ordering Help

- Always specify **size, brand, and fit category (Men's/Women's/Unisex/Youth)** together — these three fields are the leading identifiers precisely because they're the most common source of a wrong-item order, especially since the same size label can mean a different actual fit across brands and categories.
- For **bulk uniform or promotional orders**, request a size run (a pre-determined mix of sizes based on expected distribution) rather than ordering a single size in bulk, unless the order is for a known, fixed group of individuals.
- Confirm **size-chart basis (body measurement vs. garment measurement)** with the vendor before a first-time bulk order — this affects fit significantly and varies by retailer, especially for online or catalog ordering without a fitting sample.
- For **recurring uniform programs**, standardize on a single brand and size-label convention where possible, since mixing brands within the same program reintroduces the sizing-inconsistency problem this convention is designed to avoid.
- For **safety or workwear-specific items** (work boots, high-visibility apparel, flame-resistant clothing), confirm the applicable safety standard or rating is met in addition to size and fit, since these items often carry compliance requirements beyond standard clothing sizing.
