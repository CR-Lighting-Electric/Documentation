## Overview

A knockout seal (also called a knockout plug or KO seal) is a closure that fills an unused knockout opening in an electrical box, panelboard, junction box, or enclosure after the metal slug has been removed, restoring the enclosure wall. It is a routine companion item to the Switch Boxes, Square Boxes, Junction Boxes, and Pull Boxes already covered in this documentation, and to any enclosure where a knockout was opened for a conduit run that was later changed, or opened by mistake.

![](/images/knockout-seal-example.png)

The requirement behind the product is NEC 110.12(A): unused openings in electrical equipment must be closed to give protection substantially equivalent to the wall of the equipment. Tape, caulk, or a loose piece of material over the hole does not meet that requirement. An open knockout also lets in moisture, condensation, pests, and debris, and is a common inspection failure.

Knockout seal types:

- **Snap-in seal** — a one-piece, spring-loaded closure that presses into the knockout and holds by tension against the hole edge; the fastest to install and the standard choice for dry indoor boxes and panels. The tradeoff is that a snap-in seal relies on spring tension alone and can loosen over time, particularly with vibration or thermal cycling.
- **Threaded plug** — a plug with a threaded body that is secured with a nut or lock ring on the inside of the enclosure; a tighter, more permanent closure that is generally preferred for enclosures in unconditioned or exposed locations. Gasketed versions add a sealing washer for moisture protection and are used with enclosures carrying a Type 3R, 4, or 4X rating, where the closure must not compromise the enclosure's rating.

Knockout seals are selected based on:

1. **Size** — matched to the **knockout hole**, not the conduit. Knockout seals are sold by conduit trade size, and each trade size corresponds to a larger hole: roughly 7/8" for 1/2" trade size, 1-1/8" for 3/4", and 1-3/8" for 1", continuing up to 2" trade size. An undersized seal leaves a gap and fails the intent of 110.12(A).
2. **Material** — Steel (zinc-plated; dry indoor boxes and panels only, since it corrodes in humid or outdoor settings), Stainless-Steel (outdoor, coastal, and corrosive locations; Type 316 for the most aggressive exposure), or Nylon (corrosion-proof plastic for indoor and outdoor use; specify a UV-stabilized grade for sun exposure, since non-stabilized plastic degrades faster).
3. **Attachment style** — Snap-In for dry indoor boxes where speed matters; Threaded for exposed locations or anywhere a more permanent, tamper-resistant closure is wanted.
4. **Environmental rating** — the closure must match the enclosure it is going into. A non-gasketed snap-in steel seal in a Type 3R, 4, or 4X enclosure defeats the enclosure's rating; a gasketed, rated plug is needed there.
5. **Listing** — most standard steel snap-in seals are UL Listed, and the listing matters for inspection; confirm the product carries the listing appropriate to the enclosure type.

## Naming Convention

When identifying knockout seals for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
SIZE TYPE (RATING) (MATERIAL) Knockout-Seal
```

### Example Names

- `1/2" Snap-In Steel Indoor Knockout-Seal`
- `3/4" Snap-In Steel Indoor Knockout-Seal`
- `1" Snap-In Steel Indoor Knockout-Seal`
- `1-1/2" Snap-In Steel Indoor Knockout-Seal`
- `1/2" Threaded NEMA-4X Nylon Knockout-Seal`
- `3/4" Threaded Stainless-Steel Knockout-Seal`
- `1" Threaded NEMA-3R Nylon Knockout-Seal`

### Convention Notes

| Descriptor     | Explanation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| SIZE           | The conduit trade size the knockout was made for (e.g., 1/2", 3/4", 1", 1-1/4", 1-1/2", 2"), which sets the hole diameter. Measure the open hole when in doubt; trade size describes the conduit, not the hole.                                 |
| TYPE           | Snap-In (spring-tension, one-piece, fastest install) or Threaded (secured with a nut or lock ring, more permanent, available gasketed).                                                                                                         |
| RATING         | Indoor (dry-location closure for standard enclosures) or the NEMA Type of the enclosure it must preserve (NEMA-3R, NEMA-4, NEMA-4X), which calls for a gasketed Threaded seal rated for that use.                                               |
| MATERIAL       | Steel (zinc-plated, dry indoor only), Stainless-Steel (outdoor, coastal, corrosive; specify 316 for the most aggressive exposure), or Nylon (corrosion-proof plastic; specify UV-stabilized for sun exposure). Default to Steel.                |

### Ordering Help

- Match the seal to the **knockout hole, not the conduit** — confirm the trade size against the opening itself, since a seal sized for the conduit instead of the hole leaves a gap.
- For **Type 3R, 4, or 4X enclosures**, use a gasketed, rated Threaded seal rather than a standard snap-in steel seal; a non-rated closure compromises the enclosure's rating even if it fills the hole.
- Use **Snap-In steel seals only in dry indoor boxes and panels**; for outdoor, coastal, washdown, or corrosive locations, step up to Stainless-Steel or UV-stabilized Nylon.
- Do not confuse knockout seals with **blank filler plates for unused breaker spaces** in panelboards — those are a separate, panelboard-specific closure governed by NEC 408.7 and are not interchangeable with a knockout seal.
- Knockout seals are a **small, easy-to-underorder companion item** — worth keeping common trade sizes (1/2in, 3/4in, 1in) stocked on the truck and in the shop rather than ordering piecemeal when an inspector flags an open knockout.
