## Overview

A slab box (in-slab floor box) is an electrical box set into a poured concrete floor during construction, providing a permanent, concrete-tight access point for power, data, or AV connections at a floor location rather than a wall. It's connected to conduit during rough-in, positioned and secured before the pour, and becomes a permanent part of the structure once the concrete cures around it — this is a similar installation logic to the Masonry Boxes already covered under Enclosures > Electrical Boxes (set in place during construction, not retrofitted), but for a horizontal floor slab rather than a vertical masonry wall, and built specifically to withstand the weight and curing pressure of wet concrete poured directly around and over it.

![](/images/slab-box-example.png)

The slab box itself is only the embedded portion; it's not the visible finished floor outlet. After the slab cures and finish flooring is installed, the box top is trimmed flush with the finished floor surface, and a separate floor box cover/receptacle assembly (not covered on this page) is attached to finish the installation and provide the actual accessible outlet.

Slab boxes are selected based on:

1. **Material:**
    - **PVC (nonmetallic)** — nonconductive, non-corroding, engineered to be concrete-tight without needing to be stuffed with paper during the pour to keep concrete out; the common lighter-duty choice for standard commercial floor construction (parkades, apartment and office buildings).
    - **Composite** — a mid-range durability option between PVC and metal.
    - **Steel/treated metal** — heavy-duty option for large-scale or higher-load projects, offering greater mechanical strength than PVC.
2. **Depth** — sized to the slab thickness and the box-fill/device needs of the final assembly; common depths run in the 3-5/8" range, though this varies by product line and slab design.
3. **Gang count** — Single or Double (multi-gang), matched to the number of devices/services the final floor outlet assembly will need to support.
4. **Hub/knockout configuration** — adjustable or fixed hub sizes for conduit connection during rough-in, sized to the conduit runs feeding the box.
5. **Mounting tabs** — slab boxes typically include both corner and traditional mounting tab options to accommodate different rebar/deck mounting approaches used by different framing and concrete crews.

## Naming Convention

When identifying slab boxes for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
GANG-SHAPE DEPTH-Depth KNOCKOUTS-Knockouts MATERIAL Slab-Box
```

### Example Names

- `2-Gang 3-5/8"-Depth 3/4"-4-Knockouts 1/2"-4-Knockouts PVC Slab-Box`
- `Round 3-5/8"-Depth 3/4"-8-Knockouts PVC Slab-Box`
- `1-Gang 3-5/8"-Depth 1/2"-2-Knockouts 3/4"-6-Knockouts PVC Slab-Box`

### Convention Notes

| Descriptor   | Explanation                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `GANG-SHAPE` | 1-Gang, 2-Gang, or Round, matched to the device/service count the finished floor outlet assembly will support.                                 |
| `DEPTH`      | The box's overall depth (e.g., 3.625in), sized to the slab thickness and box-fill needs.                                                       |
| `KNOCKOUTS`  | The conduit hub/knockout size (e.g., 1/2in, 3/4in, 1in), matched to the conduit runs feeding the box during rough-in.                          |
| `(MATERIAL)` | PVC (standard, nonconductive, concrete-tight), Composite (mid-range durability), or Steel (heavy-duty, large-scale projects). Defaults to PVC. |

### Ordering Help

- Confirm the slab box is **specifically listed for floor use** per NEC 314.27(B) — a standard wall/junction box is not a substitute, since floor boxes face different mechanical (foot traffic, furniture, cleaning equipment) and moisture exposure than wall-mounted boxes.
- Coordinate placement and conduit rough-in with the **concrete pour schedule** — like masonry boxes, a slab box is set before the pour and is very disruptive to relocate afterward; confirm final device locations are locked in before rough-in, not left to be decided during finish-out.
- Match **material to project scale and mechanical demands** — PVC is standard and adequate for most commercial floor construction; step up to composite or steel where higher mechanical load or a larger-scale project justifies it.
- Remember the **slab box and the finished cover/receptacle assembly are separate purchases** — the slab box alone provides no usable outlet; plan to order a matching cover assembly (brass, stainless steel, powder-coated, or plastic, with gasketed or hinged options for high-traffic areas) once finish flooring is specified.
- Confirm **hub sizes match the planned conduit runs** before the box is set — like other pre-set boxes on this list, correcting a hub mismatch after the pour means working around embedded concrete rather than a simple swap.
