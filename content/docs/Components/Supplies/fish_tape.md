## Overview

Fish tape is a stiff, reeled tape pushed through conduit or a wall/ceiling cavity to guide a pull line or conductor through — the tool-based counterpart to Pull Line (mule tape), suited to shorter or more obstructed runs where a fish tape's rigidity lets it be pushed and steered rather than relying on the run already having a line pre-installed or being fed some other way. See the Pull Line page for the flat-tape pulling product itself and how the two compare; this page covers the mfish tape tool.

![](/images/fish-tape-example.png)

Fish tape comes in two material families mainly with other material choices, and the choice isn't just a durability preference — it's a safety distinction:

- **Steel fish tape** — flat spring steel, wound on a reel case; strong and rigid, good pushing power through tight or twisting conduit runs, and the traditional standard. Steel is electrically conductive, which makes it unsuitable for use near or in proximity to energized conductors or equipment — a live circuit contacted by steel fish tape is a direct shock/arc-flash hazard.
- **Fiberglass fish tape (fish rod/fishstick)** — a fiberglass rod or multi-groove tape, nonconductive, used wherever there's any possibility of contact with energized parts, or where a job's practice defaults to nonconductive tools regardless. Often paired with a nylon or nonconductive tip to keep the entire working end nonconductive.

Fish tape is selected based on:

1. **Material** — Steel for general de-energized conduit/cavity work where maximum pushing rigidity is wanted; Fiberglass wherever proximity to energized equipment is possible or required by site safety practice.
2. **Width** — common widths are 1/8", 3/16", and 1/4"; narrower tape threads more easily through crowded conduit, while wider tape offers more pushing stiffness for longer or more difficult runs.
3. **Length** — commonly available from 25 ft up through 100-125 ft or more, matched to the longest run the tape needs to reach in one pull.
4. **Tip/leader type** — a bullet-nose, chamfered, or looped tip reduces catching as the tape is fed through conduit and provides an attachment point for the conductor/pull line being fished; some fiberglass fish tapes use a spiral steel leader at the working end for extra stiffness while keeping the bulk of the tape nonconductive.
5. **Case/reel type** — a reel case (steel, aluminum, or high-impact plastic) with a crank or fast-rewind mechanism for storing and retrieving the tape; footage markings (often laser-etched) let the installer measure conduit run length as the tape is fed in, the same function as pull tape's printed footage markings.

## Naming Convention

When identifying fish tape for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
LENGTH WIDTH-Width SHAPE MATERIAL (TIP) Fish-Tape
```

### Example Names


- `200' 3/16"-Width Polyester Spiral-Steel-Leader Fish-Tape`
- `125' 1/8"-Width Flat Steel Fish-Tape`
- `240' 1/8"-Width Round Nylon Flexible-Metal-Leader Fish-Tape`
- `100' 3/16"-Width Fiberglass Round Fish-Tape`

### Convention Notes

| Descriptor     | Explanation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `LENGTH`       | The tape's total length on the reel (e.g., 25ft, 50ft, 100ft), matched to the longest run needed in one pull.                                                                                                                                              |
| `WIDTH`        | The tape's width (e.g., 1/8in, 3/16in, 1/4in); narrower threads more easily through crowded conduit, wider offers more pushing stiffness.                                                                                                                  |
| `SHAPE`        | Shape of the fish tape itself; defaults to Round. Can be ordered in Flat as well.                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `MATERIAL`     | Steel (conductive, standard pushing rigidity, de-energized work only) or Fiberglass (nonconductive, required near energized parts or per site safety practice), or Polyester (for high flexibility).                                                       |
| `(TIP)`        | The tip/leader style (e.g., Slim-Plastic, Double-Loop, Nylon-Bullet, Spiral-Steel-Leader), affecting how easily the tape feeds and what it can be attached to.                                                                                             |

### Ordering Help

- Always confirm **Fiberglass over Steel** wherever there's any possibility of contact with energized conductors or equipment — this is a safety-driven material choice, not a durability preference, and steel fish tape should never be used for fishing near live parts.
- Match **length to the longest anticipated run**, not the average — a tape that's too short to complete a pull in one pass means feeding, retrieving, and re-feeding, which costs more time than simply stocking a longer tape.
- **Narrower widths (1/8")** thread more easily through crowded or smaller-diameter conduit; **wider widths (1/4")** offer more pushing stiffness for longer, straighter runs — pick based on the specific run's obstruction level, not by default habit.
- Confirm the **tip style** suits the attachment method planned — a looped tip is typically used to tie off a pull line or conductor directly, while a bullet or chamfered tip is optimized for feeding through conduit with less catching.
- Fish tape is a **reusable tool, not a consumable** — unlike Pull Line, which is often left installed in conduit, fish tape is retrieved and reused across jobs, so durability and case/rewind quality matter more here than on a one-time-use product.
