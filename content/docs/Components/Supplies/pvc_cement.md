## Overview

PVC cement (solvent cement) is the solvent-based adhesive used to make a permanent, fused joint between PVC conduit, fittings, and couplings — it doesn't glue the surfaces together so much as chemically soften and fuse the two plastic surfaces into one continuous piece as the solvent flashes off. For electrical work this is specifically PVC conduit cement, distinct from cement formulated for CPVC or ABS pipe, which use different solvent/resin chemistry and aren't interchangeable with PVC cement despite superficially similar packaging.

![](/images/pvc-cement-example.png)

PVC cement is selected based on:

1. **Body/viscosity** — Regular body flows fastest and is suited to smaller-diameter, closer-tolerance fits; Medium body is the common middle ground for typical conduit sizes; Heavy body fills larger gaps and is suited to bigger-diameter conduit where a thicker fill is needed to bridge the joint clearance.
2. **Color** — Clear and Gray are the two colors typically used for electrical conduit cement (gray for visual match/contrast against gray PVC conduit and to visibly confirm coverage during application); Orange is associated with CPVC cement, a different product not used on standard PVC electrical conduit.
3. **Set/cure speed** — Fast-Set and Medium-Set formulations affect how much working/positioning time is available after applying cement and before the joint sets; faster set speeds suit production runs, slower set speeds suit larger joints needing more time to align and seat.
4. **Diameter rating** — cement products are rated for use up to a maximum conduit diameter (e.g., up to 8"); larger conduit sizes may require a heavier-body cement or a specific product rated for that diameter.
5. **Listing/standard** — for electrical conduit specifically, confirm the product meets ASTM D2564 and is UL 651 listed and NEMA TC-2/TC-6/TC-8 compliant, since these are what code and inspection reference for electrical PVC conduit joints.

Primer is a related, separate product: a solvent-only (no resin) liquid, typically purple or clear, applied to both surfaces immediately before cementing to soften and prepare the plastic for a proper chemical bond. Some jurisdictions and specs require primer use (especially with purple primer, so the joint is visibly verifiable as primed during inspection); always confirm local requirements rather than assuming cement alone is sufficient or that primer is optional.

## Naming Convention

When identifying PVC cement for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
SIZE (BODY) (COLOR) (SET) Cement
```

### Example Names

- `8oz Regular Clear Fast-Set Cement`
- `16oz Medium Gray Fast-Set Cement`
- `32oz Medium Gray Medium-Set Cement`
- `32oz Heavy Gray Fast-Set Cement`
- `4oz Regular Clear Fast-Set Cement`
- `128oz Medium Clear Fast-Set Cement`

### Convention Notes

| Descriptor | Explanation                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `SIZE`     | The container size (e.g., 4oz, 8oz, 16oz, 32oz, 128oz/gallon).                                                                                                                                                             |
| `(BODY)`   | Regular (fastest flow, smaller-diameter/closer-tolerance joints), Medium (standard middle ground for common conduit sizes), or Heavy (thicker fill for larger-diameter joints or wider gap tolerance). Default to Regular. |
| `(COLOR)`  | Clear or Gray for standard PVC electrical conduit cement; Orange denotes a CPVC-specific product and should not be substituted for PVC electrical conduit work. Default to Gray.                                           |
| `(SET)`    | Fast-Set (shorter working time, faster production) or Medium-Set (more working time for larger or more complex joints). Default to Medium-Set.                                                                             |

### Ordering Help

- Always confirm **PVC-specific cement**, not CPVC or ABS — the resin chemistry differs, and an orange CPVC cement is not a substitute for gray/clear PVC electrical conduit cement even though the containers look similar.
- Confirm the product is **ASTM D2564-compliant and UL 651 listed** for electrical conduit use specifically, since this is what most electrical inspections reference for PVC conduit joints.
- Check the **diameter rating** against the largest conduit size being joined — cement rated for smaller diameters may not be appropriate for larger conduit runs without confirming the manufacturer's rating.
- Confirm whether **primer is required** by the local jurisdiction or project spec before ordering cement alone; where required, order primer as a matched companion product from the same manufacturer.
- Match **set speed to the job** — fast-set speeds up production runs of standard joints, but a larger or more complex joint benefits from a medium-set product that allows more time to align and fully seat the fitting before the joint sets.
