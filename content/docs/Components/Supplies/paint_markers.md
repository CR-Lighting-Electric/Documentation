## Overview

A paint marker is a pen-style applicator dispensing an opaque, quick-drying paint rather than ink, used for marking metal, concrete, conduit, equipment, and ground surfaces where a pencil or standard marker won't show up, adhere, or hold up outdoors. On electrical work they show up in two main roles: general identification/QA marking (labeling panels, conduit, equipment, or marking a completed inspection point) and utility locate marking (surface-painting the location of buried or existing electrical lines per the industry-standard color code, ahead of excavation).

![](/images/paint-marker-example.png)

Paint markers are distinguished by:

- **Activation mechanism** — **Valve-action** markers use a ball-valve tip that releases paint only when pressed against a surface (after shaking to charge the tip), giving more control and less dripping than a standard tip; **standard felt/fiber-tip** markers flow continuously once uncapped, similar to a traditional marker, and are generally lower-cost but less precise on vertical or overhead surfaces.
- **Tip size** — Fine (roughly 1/16in / 1.5mm) for detailed labeling and small components; Bullet/Standard (roughly 1/8in / 2-3mm) for general-purpose marking and most locate work; Broad for highly visible, large-scale ground marking.
- **Paint base** — Oil-based paint is the industrial standard, formulated to adhere to oily, dirty, or outdoor metal surfaces and to resist weather, fading, and handling; water-based/latex markers are lower-odor and easier to clean up but less durable on oily or rough surfaces and less weather-resistant outdoors.
- **Color** — selected either for general visibility/contrast against the marked surface, or per the **APWA Uniform Color Code** when the mark identifies a specific buried utility type ahead of excavation: Red (electric power lines, cables, conduit, and lighting cables), Yellow (gas, oil, steam, petroleum, or other flammable material), Orange (communication, alarm, or signal lines, including fiber optic), Blue (potable water), Green (sewer and drain lines), Purple (reclaimed water, irrigation, and slurry lines), Pink (temporary survey markings), and White (proposed excavation limits).

Paint markers are selected based on:

1. **Use case** — Red/APWA-coded markers for utility locate marking ahead of excavation; general-purpose colors (White, Black, Silver, Yellow) for equipment labeling, QA marks, and visibility marking that isn't tied to the locate color code.
2. **Activation mechanism** — Valve-Action for controlled, precise marking (vertical surfaces, equipment labeling, detailed component marking); Standard felt-tip for quick, continuous ground marking where precision matters less.
3. **Tip size** — Fine for small components and detailed labeling; Bullet/Standard for most general marking and locate work; Broad for large, highly visible ground marks.
4. **Paint base** — Oil-based for outdoor, oily, or long-duration marking that needs to survive weather and handling; Water-based where lower odor/easier cleanup matters more than maximum durability (indoor, short-duration marking).
5. **Surface** — all types generally work across metal, concrete, asphalt, rubber, and plastic, but oily or wet surfaces specifically call for an oil-based, oleophobic-formulated paint to get reliable adhesion.

## Naming Convention

When identifying paint markers for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
ACTIVATION TIP-SIZE PAINT-BASE COLOR Paint-Marker
```

### Example Names

- `Valve-Action Bullet Oil-Based Red Paint-Marker`
- `Valve-Action Bullet Oil-Based Yellow Paint-Marker`
- `Valve-Action Fine Oil-Based White Paint-Marker`
- `Standard Fine Oil-Based Black Paint-Marker`
- `Valve-Action Bullet Oil-Based Orange Paint-Marker`
- `Standard Bullet Water-Based Silver Paint-Marker`

### Convention Notes

|Descriptor|Explanation|
|---|---|
|ACTIVATION|Valve-Action (ball-valve tip, controlled release, less dripping on vertical/overhead surfaces) or Standard (continuous-flow felt/fiber tip, lower-cost, less precise).|
|TIP-SIZE|Fine (~1/16in, detailed labeling/small components), Bullet (~1/8in, general-purpose and locate marking), or Broad (large, highly visible ground marks).|
|PAINT-BASE|Oil-Based (industrial standard, weather- and oil-resistant, best outdoor durability) or Water-Based (lower odor/easier cleanup, best for indoor/short-duration marking).|
|COLOR|Red, Yellow, Orange, Blue, Green, Purple, Pink, or White when marking per the APWA Uniform Color Code for buried utility locates; Black, Silver, or other general-visibility colors for non-locate labeling/QA marking.|
|Catalog number|Vendor catalogs (e.g., Markal, Arro-Mark, U-Mark, McMaster-Carr) also carry their own part numbers; provide both the plain-language name and the catalog number when known.|

### Ordering Help

- When marking for **utility locates ahead of excavation**, use **Red specifically for electric power lines, cables, and conduit** per the APWA Uniform Color Code — using the wrong color risks a miscommunication with excavation crews that the color code exists specifically to prevent.
- Stock **Oil-Based markers as the default** for any outdoor, metal, or long-duration marking — the weather and oil resistance holds up far better than water-based alternatives on a jobsite, where durability matters more than easy cleanup.
- Choose **Valve-Action over Standard felt-tip** wherever marking precision matters (equipment labeling, panel/component marking, vertical or overhead surfaces) — the controlled release reduces drips and gives a cleaner mark.
- Keep a stock of **both locate-color markers (Red, Yellow, Orange, Blue) and general-purpose colors (White, Black, Silver)** separately, since the two use cases shouldn't be mixed — a locate-colored marker used for general labeling (or vice versa) risks confusion on a jobsite where the color code is actively relied on.
- For **detailed component or small-equipment labeling**, a Fine tip gives a cleaner, more legible mark than a Bullet or Broad tip intended for larger-scale ground or surface marking.
