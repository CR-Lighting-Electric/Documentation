## Overview

Tracer tape (also called underground warning tape or detectable warning tape) is a wide, printed plastic tape buried in the trench above an underground utility line — not touching the line itself — to warn anyone digging later that a utility is buried below before they reach it. It's an early-warning layer, not a locating tool on its own: it's placed at a set depth above the actual conductor or conduit so a shovel or excavator bucket exposes the tape (and its printed warning) before it reaches the line underneath.

![](/images/tracer-tape-example.png)

Tracer tape comes in two constructions:

- **Non-detectable tape** — a plain polyethylene film, printed with the warning legend, that relies entirely on being physically dug up and seen. Lower cost, but provides no way to locate the utility from the surface before digging.
- **Detectable tape** — the same printed polyethylene tape with an internal aluminum foil core (or, in some products, a tracer wire), letting a metal detector or cable locator find the utility's approximate path from the surface without excavation, in addition to the physical dig-up warning. This is the standard choice anywhere the utility itself is non-metallic (PVC conduit, plastic gas or water pipe) and wouldn't otherwise be locatable by a metal detector.

Color and printed legend follow the APWA (American Public Works Association) Uniform Color Code, so the tape identifies which utility it's warning about at a glance:

- **Red** — electric power lines, cable, and conduit.
- **Yellow** — gas, oil, steam, or other hazardous/flammable material lines.
- **Orange** — communication, alarm, or signal lines.
- **Blue** — potable water.
- **Green** — sewer and drain lines.

Tracer tape is selected based on:

1. **Detectability** — Detectable for non-metallic utilities or wherever future locating without excavation is needed; Non-Detectable where cost is the priority and the utility is otherwise locatable (e.g., metallic conduit).
2. **Color/legend** — matched to the APWA code for the utility being marked (Red for electric), with the correct printed warning legend for that utility type.
3. **Width** — wider tape is used at greater burial depths so the warning layer stays proportionally visible relative to the digging tool likely to expose it first; common widths are 2", 3", and 6".
4. **Burial depth** — tape is placed per a maximum recommended depth for its width (roughly 12" for 2" tape, 12-18" for 3" tape, 24"+ for 6" tape), always above and separate from the utility itself, with local utility/AHJ and one-call requirements taking precedence over any general rule of thumb.
5. **Roll length** — sized to the run length being trenched, with 1,000 ft the standard roll length to minimize splices on long runs.

## Naming Convention

When identifying tracer tape for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
(WIDTH) (DETECTABILITY) COLOR Tracer-Tape
```

### Example Names

- `3" Detectable Red 1000ft Tracer-Tape`
- `6" Detectable Red 1000ft Tracer-Tape`
- `3" Non-Detectable Red 1000ft Tracer-Tape`
- `2" Detectable Red 500ft Tracer-Tape`
- `3" Detectable Orange 1000ft Tracer-Tape`

### Convention Notes

| Descriptor        | Explanation                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `(WIDTH)`         | The tape's roll width (e.g., 2in, 3in, 6in), sized to the utility's burial depth — wider tape for deeper burial. Default to 4".                                                                                           |
| `(DETECTABILITY)` | Detectable (aluminum-foil-core or tracer-wire construction, locatable by metal detector before excavation) or Non-Detectable (plain polyethylene, warning by physical dig-up only). Default to Detectable.                |
| `COLOR`           | Red (electric, per APWA color code) is standard for this catalog's use, though Yellow (gas/hazardous), Orange (communication), Blue (water), and Green (sewer) exist for other utility types the same tape family covers. |

## Typical Units of Measure

|Unit|Typical Use|
|---|---|
|**Roll**|Standard individual unit, most commonly **1,000 ft per roll**; shorter rolls (e.g., 500 ft) also available.|
|**Case**|Distributor bulk quantity for large commercial, utility, or underground infrastructure projects; multiple rolls per case.|

### Ordering Help

- Always specify **detectable vs. non-detectable** — detectable tape is the appropriate default wherever the buried utility itself (PVC conduit, plastic pipe) can't be found by a metal detector on its own; non-detectable tape only provides a warning at the point of physical excavation.
- Confirm **color and printed legend match the utility** — Red with an electric-specific legend for power/communication conduit runs, per the APWA Uniform Color Code, so future excavators anywhere in the region get a consistent warning regardless of which utility placed the tape.
- Match **width to burial depth** — deeper burial calls for wider tape so the warning stays proportionally effective relative to the excavation depth at which it's likely to be uncovered; confirm depth and width against the local utility or AHJ's specific requirements, which take precedence over general guidelines.
- Tracer tape is placed **above the utility, never in contact with it** — it's a warning layer in the backfill, not a wrap, sleeve, or protective covering for the conductor or conduit itself.
- Standard **1,000 ft rolls** minimize splices on long trenched runs; order shorter rolls only for short or irregular runs where a full roll would go mostly unused.
