## Overview

Caulk is a flexible sealant applied to fill gaps, joints, and penetrations — sealing out air, water, and sound, or, in the fire-rated case, sealing a penetration against the spread of flame and smoke. On electrical work it shows up most often sealing conduit and cable penetrations through exterior walls and roofs (weatherproofing), and sealing conduit, cable, and box penetrations through fire-rated walls, floors, and ceilings (firestopping) — two distinct applications that call for very different products and are not interchangeable.

![](/images/caulk-example.png)

Caulk types:

- **Silicone** — highly flexible, weather- and UV-resistant, with the best long-term durability of the general sealant types (commonly rated up to 20 years); the standard choice for exterior weatherproofing around conduit and equipment penetrations, though traditional silicone isn't paintable (paintable silicone/silicone-blend formulations exist where a painted finish is needed).
- **Acrylic latex** — inexpensive, water-cleanup, fully paintable, but less durable and less weather-resistant than silicone; suited to interior gap-sealing (trim, drywall penetrations) rather than exterior or wet-location use.
- **Polyurethane (expanding foam)** — expands to fill larger gaps and openings around conduit, pipe, and outlet penetrations, with good insulating and sound-dampening properties; cures over roughly 10 days and is paintable once cured. Not a substitute for a fire-rated product at a fire-rated penetration, even though it's commonly used for general gap-filling around the same kinds of penetrations.
- **Butyl rubber** — an exterior-only specialty sealant with strong water resistance and wide temperature tolerance, used for roofing, flashing, gutters, and metal-to-metal exterior seals rather than general caulking.
- **Firestop sealant** — a specialized, UL-listed product (UL 1479 / ASTM E814) applied in the annular space around conduit, cable, and penetrations through fire-rated walls, floors, and ceilings; most firestop sealants use intumescent chemistry that expands when exposed to heat (roughly 400°F+) to seal the opening against flame and smoke passage. Firestop sealant must be installed as part of a tested, listed firestop system (a specific combination of substrate, penetrant type, annular space, and sealant depth) — the sealant alone, applied outside a listed system configuration, does not provide a code-compliant fire rating.

Caulk is selected based on:

1. **Application** — Silicone or Butyl-Rubber for exterior weatherproofing, Acrylic-Latex for interior non-wet gap-sealing, Polyurethane for larger general-purpose gap-filling, Firestop specifically and only for fire-rated wall/floor/ceiling penetrations.
2. **Fire rating (firestop sealant only)** — matched to the fire-resistance rating of the assembly being penetrated (commonly 1-hour or 2-hour), and installed per the specific listed firestop system the rating is based on.
3. **Color** — Clear or White for general sealant work where appearance matters; Firestop sealant is typically Red (a visual/inspection cue identifying it as a fire-rated product, distinct from general-purpose caulk).
4. **Cartridge/package size** — matched to the gun equipment on hand and the volume of the job (standard cartridge vs. larger sausage/bulk packaging).
5. **Cure/working time** — relevant where a penetration needs to be closed up or made weathertight on a tight schedule; polyurethane foam's ~10-day full cure is a scheduling consideration general caulk doesn't carry.

## Naming Convention

When identifying caulk for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
TYPE RATING COLOR SIZE Caulk
```

### Example Names

- `Silicone NA Clear 10.1oz Caulk`
- `Silicone NA White 10.1oz Caulk`
- `Acrylic-Latex NA White 10.1oz Caulk`
- `Polyurethane-Foam NA Gray 12oz Caulk`
- `Butyl-Rubber NA Black 10.1oz Caulk`
- `Firestop 1-Hour Red 10.1oz Caulk`
- `Firestop 2-Hour Red 29oz Caulk`

### Convention Notes

| Descriptor     | Explanation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| TYPE           | Silicone (exterior weatherproofing, flexible/UV-resistant), Acrylic-Latex (interior, paintable), Polyurethane-Foam (expanding, general gap-filling), Butyl-Rubber (exterior specialty, roofing/flashing), or Firestop (UL-listed, fire-rated penetrations only). |
| RATING         | For Firestop sealant, the fire-resistance rating of the assembly it's listed for (e.g., 1-Hour, 2-Hour), matched to the specific listed firestop system in use; NA for all non-firestop types.                                                                   |
| COLOR          | Clear, White, Gray, or Black for general sealant types; Firestop sealant is typically Red as a visual identifier distinguishing it from general-purpose caulk.                                                                                                   |
| SIZE           | The cartridge/package size (e.g., 10.1oz standard cartridge, 29oz sausage/bulk pack), matched to the gun equipment on hand and job volume.                                                                                                                       |
| Catalog number | Vendor catalogs (e.g., Hilti, 3M, Dow, Grainger) also carry their own part numbers; provide both the plain-language name and the catalog number when known, since exact listed-system compatibility is manufacturer- and product-specific for firestop sealant.  |

### Ordering Help

- Never substitute **general-purpose caulk for firestop sealant** at a fire-rated penetration, and never substitute a different manufacturer's firestop sealant into a listed system it wasn't tested as part of — both break the code-compliant fire rating even if the penetration looks sealed.
- Match the **firestop sealant's rating and installation method to the specific listed system** called for (substrate type, penetrant, annular space, and sealant depth) — confirm against the manufacturer's listed system documentation before ordering or installing, not just the hourly rating alone.
- Use **Silicone for exterior conduit and equipment penetrations** rather than acrylic latex, which isn't durable enough for sustained weather exposure; reserve acrylic latex for interior, non-wet gap-sealing where paintability matters.
- Account for **polyurethane foam's ~10-day full cure time** when scheduling work that depends on the sealed penetration being complete (e.g., before final weatherproofing inspection or exterior finish work).
- Stock **Red firestop sealant separately from general-purpose caulk colors** so crews don't inadvertently grab the wrong product at a fire-rated penetration — the color difference is also what an inspector will look for.
