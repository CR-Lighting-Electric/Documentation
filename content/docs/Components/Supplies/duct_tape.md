## Overview

Duct tape is the general-purpose, cloth-backed utility tape kept on hand for temporary repairs, bundling, surface protection, marking, and any number of quick fixes on the job — not to be confused with the actual sealing of HVAC ductwork, which despite the name is a job for foil (metalized) tape or mastic, not standard cloth duct tape. Standard duct tape is a woven fabric mesh, coated with a polyethylene film and a rubber-based pressure-sensitive adhesive; the cloth backing gives it tear-by-hand usability and reasonable strength, while the poly coating provides a degree of water resistance.

![](/images/duct-tape-example.png)

Duct tape is graded by build quality more than by a formal classification standard:

- **General-purpose (light/economy grade)** — a lower fabric count, thinner poly film, and lighter adhesive coating; adequate for short-term, low-stress fixes but not for anything load-bearing or long-lived.
- **Professional/contractor grade** — a heavier fabric count, thicker film, and stronger adhesive than general-purpose; the standard choice for daily job-site use where the tape needs to actually hold under some load or weather exposure.
- **Industrial grade** — the heaviest-duty cloth tape build, with additional adhesive coat weight and reinforced backing, used for demanding or high-load applications beyond typical daily use.

A separate, unrelated product sometimes confused with duct tape by name is **true HVAC/foil tape** — a metalized (aluminum) or UL 181-listed tape specifically constructed and tested for sealing HVAC duct joints and seams. Cloth duct tape's adhesive dries out and fails over time when exposed to the heat and airflow inside actual ductwork, which is why mechanical and energy codes generally require a listed foil or mastic product for that application rather than cloth tape, regardless of what the cloth product is commonly called. This page covers cloth duct tape as a general job-site supply item; HVAC sealing tape is a distinct product not covered here.

Duct tape is selected based on:

1. **Grade** — General-Purpose for light, short-term use; Contractor-Grade for standard daily job-site use; Industrial-Grade for heavier-duty or higher-load applications.
2. **Width** — most commonly 2" for hand use; wider (3" or more) for faster coverage on larger repairs or bundling jobs.
3. **Thickness (mil)** — heavier mil generally correlates with a stronger fabric/adhesive build and better tear/puncture resistance.
4. **Color** — silver/gray is the standard, general-purpose default; other colors are used for job-site marking, identification, or matching a surface, with no difference in adhesive performance by color alone.
5. **Environment** — outdoor or prolonged exposure calls for a UV-resistant or weather-rated product; standard duct tape's adhesive degrades faster under sustained sun or moisture exposure than a purpose-built outdoor tape.

## Naming Convention

When identifying duct tape for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
(WIDTH) (THICKNESS) (GRADE) COLOR Duct-Tape
```

### Example Namesxs

- `2" 9mil Contractor-Grade Silver Duct-Tape`
- `3" Industrial-Grade Black Duct-Tape`
- `2" Red Duct-Tape`
- `Blue Duct-Tape`

### Convention Notes

| Descriptor    | Explanation                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `(GRADE)`     | General-Purpose (light/economy, short-term use), Contractor-Grade (standard daily job-site strength and adhesion), or Industrial-Grade (heaviest-duty build, higher load/adhesion). Default to General-Purpose. |
| `(WIDTH)`     | The tape's roll width, most commonly 2" for general hand use or 3" for faster coverage on larger jobs. Default to 2".                                                                                           |
| `(THICKNESS)` | The tape's gauge in mils (e.g., 7mil general-purpose, 9mil contractor-grade, 11mil+ industrial-grade); heavier mil generally means a stronger fabric/adhesive build. Default to 7mil.                           |
| `COLOR`       | Silver (standard general-purpose default) or another color (Black, White, Yellow, etc.) for job-site marking or identification purposes.                                                                        |

## Typical Units of Measure

|Unit|Typical Use|
|---|---|
|**Roll**|Standard individual unit, sold by length (commonly **45-60 yd per roll** depending on width and grade).|
|**Pack**|Small-quantity packaging, commonly **3-6 rolls per pack**.|
|**Case**|Distributor bulk quantity for large commercial or industrial projects, commonly **24 rolls per case**.|

### Ordering Help

- Never substitute cloth duct tape for **HVAC duct sealing** — if the job calls for sealing an actual duct joint or seam, order a UL 181-listed foil tape or mastic product instead; cloth duct tape's adhesive is not rated to hold up under sustained duct heat and airflow.
- Match **grade to duty cycle** — general-purpose tape is fine for a quick, short-lived fix, but anything expected to hold under load, weather, or time should go to contractor or industrial grade.
- For **outdoor or prolonged exposure**, confirm the specific product is UV-resistant or weather-rated; standard duct tape's adhesive will dry out and fail faster than a purpose-built outdoor tape under sustained sun exposure.
- **Silver/gray** covers the large majority of general use; stock a secondary color only when job-site marking or identification calls for it.
- Confirm **width and mil thickness together** — a wider roll doesn't necessarily mean a stronger build; check the actual mil rating against the load or task rather than assuming width alone indicates duty grade.
