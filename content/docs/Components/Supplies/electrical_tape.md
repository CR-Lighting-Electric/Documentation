## Overview

Electrical tape is a pressure-sensitive tape used to insulate splices and terminations, bundle and identify conductors, and provide mechanical protection where a permanent, code-recognized covering is needed but a heat-shrink or molded cover isn't practical. Several distinct tape types share the name, each built for a different job.

![](/images/electrical-tape-example.png)

- **Vinyl (PVC) tape** — the everyday general-purpose type, recognized as a primary electrical insulation by UL 510; flexible, stretches to conform around a splice, and available in a full color range for both insulation and phase/conductor identification. This is what's meant by "electrical tape" in most day-to-day use.
- **Rubber splicing tape** — a self-fusing (self-vulcanizing) rubber tape with no adhesive backing; it bonds to itself rather than to the substrate, forming a continuous rubber insulation layer over a splice that's then typically covered with an outer layer of vinyl tape for mechanical/UV protection. Used where a higher-voltage splice needs a true rubber insulation buildup rather than just a covering.
- **Friction tape** — a cloth tape impregnated with a moisture-resistant adhesive compound, historically used as a protective outer wrap over rubber splicing tape; largely superseded by vinyl tape for that role today, but still specified in some older or industrial rewrap applications.
- **High-temperature tape** — glass cloth or specialty film tape rated well above vinyl's typical service range, used on motor leads, high-temperature equipment, or anywhere near a heat source that would soften or degrade standard vinyl tape.
- **Phase/marking tape** — vinyl tape used specifically for color-coding conductors and phase identification per NEC and site standards (see Convention Notes), rather than for insulating a splice.

Electrical tape is selected based on:

1. **Tape type** — Vinyl for general splicing/insulation/marking, Rubber-Splicing for a self-fusing rubber insulation buildup on higher-voltage splices, Friction for a cloth outer protective wrap, or High-Temp for elevated-temperature service.
2. **Width** — common widths are 3/4" (the standard for hand-wrapping splices and terminations) and 2" (bundling, larger-diameter wraps, and faster coverage on bigger jobs); other widths exist for specialty use.
3. **Thickness (mil)** — vinyl tape is commonly available from roughly 7 mil (general purpose) up to 8.5-10+ mil (heavier-duty, better conformability and puncture resistance); UL 510 sets minimum dielectric and adhesion requirements regardless of thickness.
4. **Color** — black for general insulation; the full color range (red, blue, white, green, yellow, orange, brown, gray, purple/violet) for phase marking, conductor identification, or site-specific color-coding standards.
5. **Temperature rating** — standard vinyl tape is typically rated for a moderate continuous-use range; high-temperature applications require a tape specifically rated for the expected service temperature, not standard vinyl pushed past its rating.

## Naming Convention

When identifying electrical tape for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
(TYPE) (WIDTH) (THICKNESS) COLOR Electrical-Tape
```

### Example Names

- `Vinyl 3/4" 7mil Black Electrical-Tape`
- `Vinyl 3/4" 7mil Red Electrical-Tape`
- `Vinyl 2" 8.5mil Black Electrical-Tape`
- `Rubber-Splicing 3/4" 30mil Black Electrical-Tape`
- `High-Temp 3/4in 7mil Black Electrical-Tape`
- `Green Electrical-Tape`

### Convention Notes

| Descriptor    | Explanation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `(TYPE)`      | Vinyl (general-purpose PVC, UL 510), Rubber-Splicing (self-fusing rubber, no adhesive), Friction (cloth outer wrap), or High-Temp (glass cloth/specialty film for elevated-temperature service). Default to Vinyl.                                                   |
| `(WIDTH)`     | The tape's roll width, most commonly 3/4in (standard hand-wrapping) or 2in (bundling/larger coverage); other widths exist for specialty use. Default to 3/4".                                                                                                        |
| `(THICKNESS)` | The tape's gauge in mils (e.g., 7mil general-purpose vinyl, 8.5mil or heavier-duty vinyl, 30mil typical for rubber splicing tape); thicker tape generally offers better conformability and puncture resistance. Default to 7mil.                                     |
| `COLOR`       | Black (general insulation, most common) or a specific identification color (Red, Blue, White, Green, Yellow, Orange, Brown, Gray, Purple) when the tape is used for phase marking or conductor identification per NEC 200.6/310.110 or a site color-coding standard. |

### Ordering Help

- Always specify **tape type** first — general-purpose vinyl is not a substitute for rubber splicing tape on a higher-voltage splice, and standard vinyl should not be used past its rated temperature range where a high-temp tape is called for.
- When tape is being used for **phase marking or conductor identification** rather than insulation, confirm the required color scheme against NEC 200.6/310.110 and any project- or site-specific color-coding standard before ordering, since color conventions can vary by region and by facility.
- **3/4" width** covers the large majority of splice-wrapping and general use; **2" width** is worth stocking separately for bundling and faster coverage on larger conductors or cable groups.
- For rubber splicing applications, remember the **rubber tape provides the insulation buildup and the vinyl (or friction) tape provides the mechanical/UV protective outer wrap** — the two are typically ordered and used together, not as substitutes for each other.
- Confirm **mil thickness** against the application; a heavier-mil vinyl tape stretches and conforms differently than a lighter general-purpose roll, which matters when wrapping irregular splice shapes.
