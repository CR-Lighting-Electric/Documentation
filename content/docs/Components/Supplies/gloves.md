## Overview

Work gloves are general-purpose hand protection worn for grip, abrasion resistance, and cut/impact protection during routine tool use and material handling — distinct from voltage-rated rubber insulating gloves (a separate, ASTM D120/Class-rated electrical safety product not covered on this page), which are selected by voltage class rather than the characteristics below and used specifically for work on or near energized conductors.

![](/images/gloves-example.png)

Work gloves are selected based on:

1. **Material/style:**
    - **Leather** — the standard heavy-duty choice, offering abrasion resistance, heat resistance, and durability for construction, rigging, and general rough handling.
    - **Synthetic/mechanic-style** — synthetic leather or knit-synthetic blends (often paired with a hook-and-loop wrist closure), lighter and more breathable than full leather, favored where dexterity for tool use matters as much as protection.
    - **Coated (dipped)** — a knit or shell glove with a Nitrile, Latex, or Polyurethane coating on the palm/fingers for grip and puncture/tear resistance; nitrile offers the highest puncture/tear resistance of the three, latex offers the most flexibility, and polyurethane balances grip and dexterity.
2. **Cut resistance (ANSI/ISEA 105 level)** — rated A1 (lowest) through A9 (highest); general handling and light assembly work is typically fine at A1-A2, while work involving sharp metal edges, glass, or blade-adjacent tasks calls for A4-A5 or higher, per OSHA guidance for metal-cutting-adjacent work.
3. **Insulation** — uninsulated for standard use, or insulated (commonly with a synthetic fill such as Thinsulate or a similar material) for cold-weather work, chosen based on season and site conditions rather than as a year-round default.
4. **Size** — sized from XX-Small through XXX-Large (with separate women's sizing on some lines), fitted to the individual for both safety (a loose glove can catch on rotating equipment or reduce grip) and dexterity.
5. **Special features** — touchscreen-capable fingertips, impact-resistant padding (e.g., knuckle protection), or flame-resistant construction for tasks with a specific added hazard beyond standard cut/abrasion protection.

## Naming Convention

When identifying work gloves for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
SIZE BRAND MATERIAL CUT-LEVEL INSULATION Gloves
```

### Example Names

- `Large Mechanix Synthetic A2 Uninsulated Gloves`
- `X-Large Mechanix Leather A1 Uninsulated Gloves`
- `Medium Mechanix Coated-Nitrile A4 Uninsulated Gloves`
- `Large Mechanix Coated-Nitrile A2 Insulated Gloves`
- `XX-Large Mechanix Leather A1 Insulated Gloves`
- `Small Mechanix Synthetic A3 Uninsulated Gloves`

### Convention Notes

| Descriptor     | Explanation                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| SIZE           | 2X-Small through 3X-Large (or women's Small/Medium/Large where applicable), fitted to the individual. Leads the name per this category's established SIZE-first convention.                                                        |
| BRAND          | The manufacturer (e.g., Mechanix, Ironclad, Youngstown, MCR Safety, Carhartt); second field per this category's convention.                                                                                                        |
| MATERIAL       | Leather (heavy-duty, abrasion/heat resistant), Synthetic (lighter, dexterity-focused), or Coated-Nitrile / Coated-Latex / Coated-Polyurethane (dipped palm/finger coating; specify which coating).                                 |
| CUT-LEVEL      | The ANSI/ISEA 105 cut-resistance rating (A1 through A9); select based on the sharp-edge/blade hazard level of the work, not as a default.                                                                                          |
| INSULATION     | Insulated (cold-weather synthetic fill) or Uninsulated (standard).                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Catalog number | Vendor catalogs also carry their own part numbers/style numbers; provide both the plain-language name and the catalog number when known, since exact cut-level rating and fit vary by specific product line even within one brand. |

### Ordering Help

- Always confirm this page is the right fit for the task — **voltage-rated rubber insulating gloves** are a separate, safety-critical product line (selected by ASTM D120 Class/voltage rating, not by the characteristics here) and must be ordered separately whenever work involves proximity to energized conductors; standard work gloves provide no electrical protection.
- Match **cut-resistance level to the actual hazard**, not habit — OSHA guidance points to A5 or higher for metal-cutting-adjacent work, while general handling and assembly is typically fine at A1-A2; over-specifying trades away dexterity, under-specifying risks injury.
- Confirm **sizing per individual** rather than ordering a single bulk size for a crew — a loose-fitting glove reduces grip and can catch on rotating or moving equipment, while an overly tight glove reduces dexterity and causes fatigue.
- Choose **coating type by task**: nitrile for the best puncture/tear resistance (manufacturing, general trades), latex for flexibility-focused precision work, polyurethane for a balance of grip and dexterity in construction/warehousing settings.
- Stock **insulated gloves seasonally** rather than year-round by default — order based on the season and site conditions the crew is actually working in.
