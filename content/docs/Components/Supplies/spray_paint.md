## Overview

Paint cans here refers to standard aerosol spray paint used for equipment touch-up, enclosure finishing, and general safety color-coding — distinct from the Paint Markers page (precision pen-style marking for labeling and locates) and from inverted/upside-down marking paint (a separate ground-layout product sprayed nozzle-down for utility locate striping, not covered on this page). The most common electrical-trade use is color-matched touch-up on painted steel enclosures, panels, and equipment where the factory finish has been scratched, drilled, or otherwise damaged during installation — along with general-purpose rust-inhibitive and safety-color coating.

![](/images/spray-paint-example.png)

Paint can types:

- **Enamel (general-purpose)** — a durable, gloss or semi-gloss finish for general metal coating and touch-up; the standard choice for most equipment and enclosure work.
- **Rust-inhibitive/primer** — formulated to bond to bare or lightly rusted steel and inhibit further corrosion, used as a base coat before a finish coat or as standalone protection on fabricated steel, strut, and support hardware.
- **Equipment touch-up (color-matched)** — spray paint formulated and color-matched to a specific manufacturer's enclosure finish, most commonly **ANSI 61 gray**, the standard factory color for electrical switchgear, panelboards, and enclosures; used to touch up scratches, drilled holes, or field modifications without leaving a visibly mismatched repair.
- **Safety/identification color** — spray paint in standardized colors used to mark hazards, equipment status, or safety-related items, generally following OSHA 29 CFR 1910.144 and ANSI Z535.1 color conventions (see Convention Notes).

Paint cans are selected based on:

1. **Type** — Enamel for general-purpose coating/touch-up, Rust-Inhibitive/primer for bare or corroding steel, Equipment-Touch-Up where a specific factory finish (most often ANSI 61 gray) needs to be matched, or Safety-Color for hazard/status marking.
2. **Color** — matched to the equipment manufacturer's finish for touch-up work (ANSI 61 gray is the dominant standard for electrical equipment), or to the OSHA/ANSI safety color code for hazard marking (see Convention Notes).
3. **Finish** — Gloss (standard for most enclosure/equipment finishes), Satin, or Flat, matched to the surface being touched up so the repair doesn't stand out.
4. **Spray mechanism** — standard upright-spray cans for general use; inverted/"any-way" cans that spray in any orientation (including upside-down) where the application position makes an upright-only can impractical.
5. **Can size** — standard 12oz aerosol cans for most touch-up and general work; larger cans or bulk packaging for higher-volume coating jobs.

## Naming Convention

When identifying paint cans for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
TYPE FINISH COLOR SIZE Spray-Paint
```

### Example Names

- `Equipment-Touch-Up Gloss ANSI-61-Gray 12oz Spray-Paint`
- `Enamel Gloss Black 12oz Spray-Paint`
- `Rust-Inhibitive Flat Red-Oxide-Primer 12oz Spray-Paint
- `Safety-Color Gloss OSHA-Safety-Red 12oz Spray-Paint`
- `Safety-Color Gloss OSHA-Safety-Yellow 12oz Spray-Paint`
- `Enamel Satin White 12oz Spray-Paint`

| `TYPE`         | Enamel (general-purpose coating/touch-up), Rust-Inhibitive (bare/corroding steel, primer), Equipment-Touch-Up (color-matched to a manufacturer's finish), or Safety-Color (hazard/status marking per OSHA/ANSI color code).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `FINISH`       | Gloss (standard for most enclosure/equipment finishes), Satin, or Flat, matched to the surface being coated or touched up.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `COLOR`        | ANSI-61-Gray (standard electrical enclosure/switchgear finish) or another manufacturer-matched color for touch-up work; Red-Oxide-Primer for rust-inhibitive base coat; OSHA-Safety-Red (fire protection equipment, danger, emergency stop), OSHA-Safety-Yellow (caution, physical hazards), or the broader ANSI Z535.1 Orange (dangerous machine parts), Green (safety/first-aid equipment), Blue (equipment caution/do-not-operate tagging), and Purple (radiation hazards) for safety-color marking; standard colors (Black, White) for general-purpose work. |

### Ordering Help

- For **enclosure and equipment touch-up, match both the color and the manufacturer** where possible — ANSI 61 gray is a near-universal standard, but exact shade can vary slightly between manufacturers, and a mismatched touch-up is more visually obvious on a gloss finish than a flat one.
- Keep **Red and Yellow safety-color cans stocked separately** from general touch-up colors — per OSHA 1910.144, Red is reserved for fire protection equipment, danger, and emergency-stop marking, and Yellow for caution/physical-hazard marking; using them interchangeably with general-purpose color stock risks diluting their safety meaning on site.
- Use **Rust-Inhibitive primer as a base coat on bare or lightly rusted steel** before a finish coat, rather than applying enamel directly, for better adhesion and longer-term corrosion protection.
- Confirm **finish (gloss/satin/flat) matches the original surface** on any touch-up job — a gloss touch-up on a flat/satin surface (or vice versa) will stand out even if the color itself is a close match.
- This page is for **standard aerosol spray cans**; for precision marking/labeling, see the Paint Markers page, and for ground-level utility locate striping, use inverted/upside-down marking paint rather than a standard-orientation can.
