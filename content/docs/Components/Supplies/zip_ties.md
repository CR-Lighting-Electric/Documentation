## Overview

A zip tie (also called a cable tie or wire tie) is a one-piece, self-locking strap used to bundle, route, and secure cables, conduit runs, and general items together. A ratchet-style head grips a toothed strap; the strap is pulled through and cinched down, and the head's internal pawl prevents it from backing out. Beyond that basic mechanism, zip ties vary widely by material, mounting style, tensile strength, and size, and picking the wrong one for the environment or load is a common field mistake — a standard indoor nylon tie left outdoors will go brittle and snap within a season or two.

![](/images/zip-tie-example.png)

Zip ties are selected based on:

1. **Material:**
    - **Standard nylon (6/6)** — the general-purpose, indoor/dry-location default; rated for a moderate continuous temperature range and not UV-stabilized.
    - **UV-resistant (weather-resistant) nylon** — carbon-black-loaded nylon for outdoor and sun-exposed use; available essentially only in black, since the carbon additive that provides UV resistance is what makes it black.
    - **High-temperature nylon** — rated for a higher continuous-temperature range than standard nylon, used near motors, panels, or other heat-generating equipment.
    - **Stainless steel** — a metal strap-and-buckle tie for the harshest environments (high heat, chemical exposure, outdoor/underground, or where nylon's flammability or UV limits are a concern); far higher tensile strength than nylon, at a higher cost.
    - **Metal-detectable nylon** — nylon compounded with a detectable additive, used in food-processing or pharmaceutical environments where a broken tie fragment must be catchable by metal detection.
2. **Mounting style:**
    - **Standard (self-locking)** — the basic strap-through-head design, the most common style, used for general bundling.
    - **Releasable (reusable)** — includes a tab or trigger that disengages the locking pawl, allowing the tie to be loosened and reused rather than cut off.
    - **Mounting-hole (screw-mount)** — has a molded hole at the head for a screw, letting the tie itself be fastened to a surface before bundling.
    - **Push-mount / wing-mount** — has a barbed or winged base that snaps into a pre-drilled panel hole without a separate screw.
3. **Tensile (breaking) strength** — the load the tie can hold before failing, commonly graded from light-duty (roughly 18 lb) up through standard (50-75 lb), heavy-duty (120-175 lb), and extra-heavy-duty/stainless (200 lb and up); the bundle's size and weight, not just cable count, should drive this choice.
4. **Length and width** — length must clear the full bundle diameter with enough tail to cinch and trim; width scales with tensile strength (wider ties carry more load) and should be matched to the connector/head style being ordered.
5. **Color** — black is standard (and the only practical UV-resistant option); other colors are used for identification, phase marking, or system color-coding rather than for any performance difference.

## Naming Convention

When identifying zip ties for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
LENGTH (MATERIAL) (MOUNT) (STRENGTH) (COLOR) Zip-Tie
```

### Example Names

- `9" Standard-Nylon Self-Locking Standard Black Zip-Tie`
- `11" UV-Nylon Self-Locking Standard Black Zip-Tie`
- `14" UV-Nylon Self-Locking Heavy-Duty Black Zip-Tie`
- `8" Standard-Nylon Releasable Standard Natural Zip-Tie`
- `4" Standard-Nylon Mounting-Hole Light-Duty Black Zip-Tie`
- `14" Stainless-Steel Self-Locking Extra-Heavy-Duty Metal Zip-Tie`
- `6" Zip-Tie`
- `8" Releasable Zip-Tie`

### Convention Notes

| Descriptor   | Explanation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `LENGTH`     | The tie's overall length (e.g., 4in, 8in, 11in, 14in, up to 36in+ for large-bundle ties), sized to clear the bundle diameter with enough tail to cinch and trim.                                                                                                       |
| `(MATERIAL)` | Standard-Nylon (indoor/dry, general-purpose), UV-Nylon (outdoor/weather-resistant, essentially black-only), High-Temp-Nylon (elevated-temperature service), Stainless-Steel (harshest environments, highest strength), or Metal-Detectable (food/pharma environments). |
| `(MOUNT)`    | Self-Locking (standard basic head), Releasable (reusable, trigger-release), Mounting-Hole (screw-mount tab), or Push-Mount (barbed/winged panel mount). Default to Self-Locking.                                                                                       |
| `(STRENGTH)` | Light-Duty (~18 lb), Standard (~50-75 lb), Heavy-Duty (~120-175 lb), or Extra-Heavy-Duty (~200 lb+, typical of stainless); wider ties generally carry the higher strength ratings.                                                                                     |
| `(COLOR)`    | Black (standard, and the practical default for UV-resistant), Natural/White, or an identification color (Red, Blue, Yellow, Green, etc.) for color-coded systems.                                                                                                      |

### Ordering Help

- Always specify **material** against the environment — a standard indoor nylon tie used outdoors will degrade and snap under UV exposure well before a UV-resistant tie would; don't substitute standard nylon to save cost on an outdoor or rooftop run.
- Size **length to the actual bundle diameter**, not just cable count — an undersized tie won't cinch fully, and an oversized tie wastes material and leaves excess tail to trim.
- Match **tensile strength to the load being bundled**, not just habit — heavier cable bundles, especially on vertical runs or overhead supports, need a heavy-duty or extra-heavy-duty rating rather than a standard tie.
- Cable ties are a **bundling method, not a code-recognized raceway support** in most cases — where NEC support/securing requirements apply to the conductors or cable themselves, confirm whether a listed support method (strap, clamp, hanger) is required instead of or in addition to ties.
- **Releasable ties** cost more per unit but pay off anywhere bundles are expected to be reopened for maintenance or moves/adds/changes, avoiding repeated cut-and-replace cycles.
