## Overview

Wire nuts (also called wire connectors, twist-on connectors, or cable nuts) are insulated fasteners used to splice two or more conductors together outside of a junction box's terminals, most commonly inside outlet boxes, junction boxes, and panels. A wire nut consists of a color-coded, flame-retardant plastic shell with an internal tapered metal spring insert; twisting the shell over stripped, aligned conductor ends draws the spring down around them, creating a mechanically secure, insulated electrical connection.

![](/images/wire-nuts.png)

Wire nuts are selected based on:

1. **Wire gauge range and count** — every wire nut is rated for a specific combination of conductor sizes and quantities (e.g., "2–3 #12 AWG" or "min. 2 #14, max. 4 #12"). This rating, printed on the packaging, is the only reliable basis for selection.
2. **Color** — <cite index="16-1">standard colors — gray, blue, orange, yellow, tan, and red — denote a general progression of conductor capacity, from smallest to largest</cite>. However, <cite index="12-1">color-to-size associations are industry conventions rather than mandatory standards, and cross-brand comparisons are unreliable</cite> — the wire range printed on the specific product's packaging always governs, not the color alone.
3. **Connection style:**
    - **Twisting**— the traditional style; the installer twists the shell by hand to engage the internal spring.
    - **Winged** — a twist-on variant with molded wings for extra grip and leverage during installation.
4. **Conductor material compatibility** — standard wire nuts are rated for copper-to-copper connections only. <cite index="13-1">aluminum wire must never be connected to copper wire with a standard wire nut, since this causes galvanic corrosion and a fire hazard; special purple wire nuts rated for aluminum-to-copper (Al/Cu) connections</cite>, typically containing an antioxidant paste, are required for that application.

**Installation notes worth flagging in the field:**

- <cite index="16-1">Overstuffing a wire nut beyond its rated capacity risks a loose connection and overheating, while underfilling it can prevent the wires from making secure contact</cite> — both are common causes of connection failure, not just one or the other.
- <cite index="13-1">Over-stripping conductors leaves bare copper exposed below the nut (a shock and short-circuit hazard), while under-stripping lets insulation enter the spring and prevents good contact</cite>.
- Taping over a properly matched, correctly installed wire nut is not a substitute for choosing the right size, and is not recommended as a way to compensate for a loose or damaged connection.

## Naming Convention

When identifying wire nuts for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
COLORS (STYLE) (RANGE) Wire-Nut 
```

| Descriptor | Explanation                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `COLORS`   | Color or combination colors of the wire nuts, i.e. "Blue" or "Tan-Red."                                                                               |
| `(STYLE)`  | Optional style of the nut, options might include "Winged" or "Straight."                                                                              |
| `(RANGE)`  | Optional range specifier of the nuts, i.e. "2-4x12-AWG" meaning two to four 12 AWG wire inputs is the range that these nuts should be able to handle. |

### Example Names

- `Tan-Red Wire-Nuts`
- `Blue Winged Wire-Nuts`
- `Yellow Winged 4-6x8-AWG Wire-Nuts`

## Typical Units of Measure

| Unit          | Typical Use                                                                                             |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Each (EA)** | Occasionally used for specialty or large-format connectors, but uncommon as a standalone ordering unit. |
| **Bag/Pack**  | Most common small-quantity packaging, typically **25, 50, or 100 per bag**.                             |
| **Jar/Jug**   | Common bulk packaging for jobsite or truck stock, typically **100 or 500 per jar/jug**.                 |
| **Case**      | Distributor bulk quantity, made up of multiple jars or boxes; used for large project takeoffs.          |

### Ordering Help

- Always specify **color, type, metal compatibility, and wire range** together — color alone is not a reliable ordering identifier across different manufacturers.
- Bag and jar quantities vary by connector size — smaller connectors (gray, blue) are typically packaged in higher counts per unit than larger ones (red, tan), so confirm actual piece count per package when comparing vendor pricing.
