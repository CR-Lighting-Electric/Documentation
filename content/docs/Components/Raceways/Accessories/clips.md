## Overview

A conduit clip — nicknamed a "bat wing" on the field for the two spring-steel wings that flare out from its body — is a one-piece spring-steel fastener used to attach conduit, armored cable (MC/AC), or communication cable directly to a mounting point without drilling, welding, or a separate strap and screw. The name traces back to a 1959 brand product; it has since become genericized across manufacturers the same way "Minnie" has for U-shaped conduit hangers. The clip's flared wings squeeze open to snap over the mounting point and close back down to grip it, while the opposite end forms a round-bodied loop or saddle that the conduit or cable is pressed into and held by spring tension.

![](/images/conduit-clip-example.png)

What distinguishes a conduit clip from a strap or hanger is the mounting side, not the conduit side. The same basic clip body is made in variants suited to snap onto three different mounting points:

1. **Flange mount** — squeezes onto the flange of a structural member (a bar joist, purlin, or the flange of a piece of steel) up to a maximum flange thickness; no drilling or welding to structural steel required. This is the classic "bat wing" application and the fastest way to hang conduit from open steel.
2. **Rod mount** — clips onto a length of threaded rod (commonly 1/4" or 3/8") hanging from an anchor above, letting the conduit run be positioned at whatever height the rod is cut to.
3. **Wire mount** — clips onto a length of ceiling support wire (the same wire used for suspended-ceiling grids), a common option in plenum spaces already carrying wire runs for other trades.

On the conduit side, each clip size is rated for a specific combination of EMT size, rigid/IMC size, and MC/AC cable range — the clip body's inner saddle diameter sets a maximum outer-diameter (OD) capacity, so one clip model is often listed as fitting, for example, 1/2" EMT _and_ 1/2" rigid _and_ a range of MC/AC cable, since those all fall within the same OD window even though the trade sizes don't literally match. This is a routine point of confusion when ordering, so conduit-clip catalogs are read by max OD capacity, not by trade size alone.

Conduit clips are selected based on:

1. **Mount type** — Flange, Rod, or Wire, matched to what's actually available to clip onto at the point of installation.
2. **Conduit/cable capacity** — the trade size(s) of EMT, rigid conduit, or MC/AC cable the clip's saddle accepts, expressed as a maximum OD.
3. **Flange thickness range** (flange-mount clips only) — the clip must be rated for the actual thickness of the structural flange it will grip; too thick and the wings won't close enough to hold.
4. **Material and finish** — spring steel is standard; electro-galvanized or zinc-plated finishes are typical for interior dry use, with stainless steel available for corrosive or damp environments.
5. **Load rating** — a light-duty rating suited to supporting the conduit or cable run itself, not for supporting personnel weight or serving as the sole support for heavier raceway, which should instead use a strap, clamp, or hanger rated for the load.

## Naming Convention

When identifying conduit clips for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
(SIZE) (MOUNT) TYPE MATERIAL Conduit-Clip
```

### Example Names

- `1/2" Flange EMT-Rigid-MC Electro-Galvanized Conduit-Clip`
- `3/4" EMT-Rigid-MC Electro-Galvanized Conduit-Clip`
- `1" Rod EMT-Rigid Electro-Galvanized Conduit-Clip`
- `1/2" EMT-Rigid-MC Electro-Galvanized Conduit-Clip`
- `1" EMT-Rigid Conduit-Clip`
- `1-1/2" MC Conduit-Clip`

### Convention Notes

| Descriptor   | Explanation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `(SIZE)`     | The nominal conduit trade size the clip's saddle is rated for (e.g., 1/2in, 3/4in, 1in), used as the ordering reference size even though actual capacity is by max OD across several conduit/cable types. If left off, and type is MC, assume 3/4" |
| `(MOUNT)`    | Flange, Rod, or Wire — which side of the clip the mounting-side wings are made to grip. Leads the name, since this determines whether the clip can even be used at a given point of installation. Assume Wire as the default.                      |
| `TYPE`       | The specific conduit and cable types the saddle accepts at that size, such as EMT-Rigid-MC (EMT, rigid/IMC, and MC/AC cable) or EMT-Rigid where cable capacity isn't listed for that model.                                                        |
| `(MATERIAL)` | Electro-Galvanized (standard interior/dry spring steel) or Stainless-Steel (damp, corrosive, or exterior locations). Assume Steel default.                                                                                                         |

### Ordering Help

- Always specify **mount type, size, and conduit/cable capacity** together — a clip's saddle is often rated for several conduit and cable types at once (e.g., EMT, rigid, and MC/AC), so ordering by trade size alone can return a clip sized for the wrong actual OD.
- For flange-mount clips, confirm the **flange thickness** of the structural member being clipped to; wings sized for a thin flange won't close on a thicker one, and vice versa.
- Conduit clips are a **light-duty, no-drill fastening method** — they're well suited to individual conduit or cable runs off open steel, ceiling wire, or threaded rod, but a heavier raceway run, gang of conduits, or any load-bearing application should use a strap, beam clamp, or hanger instead.
- The "bat wing" nickname is used interchangeably for the whole clip family in the field regardless of manufacturer; when matching an existing installation, confirm the actual mount type and OD capacity rather than ordering by nickname alone.
- Match **finish to environment** — standard electro-galvanized for interior dry use, stainless steel for damp, washdown, or corrosive locations.
