## Overview

Clamps are the fittings that attach raceway, threaded rod, strut, or a support point directly to a structural steel member or to a pipe/conduit run without drilling, welding, or otherwise altering the structure — most commonly a **beam clamp** gripping a steel beam flange, or a **pipe clamp** securing conduit or pipe to strut, a beam, or another flat surface. This page covers both families, since they're frequently specified together on the same support run (a beam clamp establishing the overhead attachment point, feeding a threaded rod down to a pipe clamp or strut assembly that actually carries the raceway).

![](/images/clamp-example.png)

**Beam clamps** grip a structural steel beam flange to create an attachment point for threaded rod, strut channel, or pipe — with no drilling or welding required, which keeps the structural member intact and the installation fully reversible. The jaws grip the flange and a set screw or bolt locks the clamp in place. Common types:

- **C-clamp (set-screw hanger clamp)** — the smallest, most common style; a malleable or ductile iron casting that tightens onto the flange with a cup-pointed set screw and has a tapped hole for threaded rod. Used to hang pipe runs, conduit, cable tray, and ductwork. **Important distinction:** a very similarly named "C-style structural beam clamp" exists as a separate, load-tested steel-to-steel connection product with published tensile/lateral ratings — the two share a name but are not the same product, and specifying the wrong one either overpays for a rod hanger or, worse, hangs a structural load from a light-duty services clamp not rated for it.
- **Universal / I-beam clamp** — accommodates a range of flange thicknesses using adjustable jaws secured by a central threaded spindle; suited to medium and heavy-duty rod hanging.
- **U-bolt beam clamp** — used when strut channel needs to span across the beam rather than a single threaded rod dropping from it.
- **Window bracket beam clamp** — has a cutout ("window") that strut channel inserts directly into, secured by a bolt through the top of the clamp; commonly used to suspend a run of strut from the underside of a steel frame.
- **Right-angle / side beam attachment clamp** — attaches conduit or pipe directly to the side of a beam flange rather than suspending from underneath it.

**Pipe/conduit clamps** secure a round raceway (conduit, tubing, or pipe) to a flat surface, strut channel, or another support member:

- **U-bolt** — two threaded arms extending from a curved base, clamping a pipe or conduit to a flat surface such as strut channel, a steel frame, or a concrete wall. Available as a **standard U-bolt** (carbon or stainless steel, zinc-plated or hot-dip galvanized) for general-purpose clamping, or an **insulated/cushioned U-bolt** with a rubber or plastic liner protecting the pipe from galvanic corrosion, vibration, and abrasion — particularly important where the raceway material differs from the clamp material.
- **Riser clamp** — supports the weight of a vertical pipe or conduit run at a floor penetration, transferring the load to the floor/slab rather than relying on lateral straps alone.

**Selection factors common to both families:**

1. **Load/duty rating** — beam clamps are rated in pounds for a given rod size and jaw configuration (e.g., a 3/8" rod C-clamp commonly rated around 400 lbs, versus a heavy-duty clamp rated up to 3,000 lbs at a larger rod size) — always size to the actual supported load, not just the rod diameter.
2. **Flange thickness capacity** — every beam clamp has a maximum (and often minimum) flange thickness it can grip; standard clamps commonly handle up to about 7/8", with wide-throat or heavy-duty clamps rated for thicker flanges.
3. **Material** — malleable or ductile iron clamps are cast and carry higher loads, suited to medium/heavy-duty applications; formed/carbon steel clamps suit lighter loads and general-purpose use; stainless steel is specified for wet, coastal, or corrosive environments.
4. **Rod/bolt size compatibility** — the clamp's tapped hole or bolt size must match the threaded rod or bolt being used to suspend or secure the load.

## Naming Convention

When identifying clamps for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:


```
SIZE (FLANGE) (MATERIAL) TYPE-Clamp
```

| Descriptor   | Explanation                                                                                |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `SIZE`       | Threaded rod, bolt size, or any other size property the clamp is tapped/sized/gripped for. |
| `(FLANGE)`   | Optional size specifier for the flange sizing if the clamp is beam style.                  |
| `(MATERIAL)` | Optional material specifier (Stainless-Steel, Aluminum, etc). Assume Steel.                |
| `TYPE`       | The actual type of clamp, i.e. Universal, U-Bolt, Riser, Beam, Conduit-Beam etc.           |

### Example Names

- `3/8" Beam-Clamp`
- `1/2" Stainless-Steel Beam-Clamp`
- `3-3/8" U-Bolt-Clamp`

|Unit|Typical Use|

|**Box**|Common bulk packaging for smaller, high-volume clamp sizes (C-clamps, standard U-bolts), typically packed **10, 25, or 50 per box**.|
|**Case**|Distributor bulk quantity for large commercial or industrial projects; multiple boxes per case.|

### Ordering Help

- Always specify **clamp type, rod/pipe size, flange thickness (for beam clamps), and material** together — a "beam clamp" or "pipe clamp" listed alone is not a complete order, since capacity and fit both depend on these fields.
- Confirm whether the application needs a **light-duty services C-clamp or a load-tested structural beam clamp** before ordering, especially where the connection will carry a calculated or engineered load — this distinction affects both cost and safety and is a common source of specification confusion.
- For **dissimilar-metal clamping** (e.g., a galvanized steel U-bolt on aluminum or PVC-coated conduit), specify an insulated/cushioned clamp to prevent galvanic corrosion and physical abrasion at the contact point.
- Verify the clamp's **rated flange thickness capacity** against the actual beam being clamped — a standard clamp will not properly seat on a flange thicker than its rated maximum, and a wide-throat or heavy-duty clamp should be specified instead.
- For **wet, coastal, or washdown environments**, specify stainless steel construction rather than a standard zinc-plated or black-oxide finish before finalizing an order.
