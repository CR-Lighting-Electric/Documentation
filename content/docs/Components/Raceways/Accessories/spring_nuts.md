## Overview

A spring nut (also called a channel nut or strut nut) is the toothed, rectangular nut used to fasten bolts, threaded rod, brackets, and fittings into strut channel — the same 1-5/8" strut system covered on the Brackets, Bases, and Clamps pages. Rather than passing a bolt through a drilled hole, a spring nut is inserted into the open slot of the channel, given a quarter turn, and seated behind the channel's inturned lips, where its teeth bite into the lip to resist pull-out and side-slip under load. An attached coil spring holds the nut in position at a set height inside the channel while the installer starts the bolt, allowing one-handed, overhead installation without needing to hold the nut in place by hand.

![](/images/spring-nut-example.png)

Spring nuts are selected based on:

1. **Spring length** — this is the single most common ordering mistake, so it's worth getting right first:
    - **Short spring** — suited to shallow channels or where extra insertion clearance is needed.
    - **Regular/standard spring** — matched to standard-depth strut channel, the most common configuration.
    - **Long spring** — holds the nut higher inside a deep channel (e.g., a 3-1/4" deep channel), making one-handed placement faster overhead or on vertical runs.
    - **No-spring** — the same threaded nut without the coil spring; costs less, but must be held in place by hand during bolt starting. Reaches the same final strength once tightened, since the spring is a holding aid only and is not a structural part of the connection.
2. **Thread size** — must match the bolt or threaded rod being used exactly (common inch threads run from #8-32 through 7/8"-9, with metric M6–M12 also available); a mismatched thread size and pitch risks cross-threading or a connection that fails under vibration.
3. **Channel width/profile compatibility** — standard nuts fit the common 1-5/8" Unistrut-style slotted channel, but not every channel profile is interchangeable; body dimensions, lip depth, and slot type must all be checked against the specific channel series before ordering, since a nut too small for the channel won't properly engage the lips and may spin or pull out, while one too large may bind and make assembly difficult.
4. **Material and finish:**
    - **Electro-galvanized (EG) zinc** — the standard finish for interior, dry-location use.
    - **Hot-dip galvanized (HDG)** — higher corrosion resistance than standard zinc, used for exterior applications.
    - **Stainless steel (304, or 316 for the most aggressive exposure)** — used for outdoor, sanitary, wet, or washdown environments; 316 specifically for coastal, chloride-rich, or chemical-processing environments where 304 would still be at risk.
5. **Case hardening** — spring nuts are press-formed and case-hardened from steel meeting standards such as ASTM A108, A575, or A36 (for larger sizes), which gives the teeth the biting strength needed to grip the channel lip securely; this hardening is a manufacturing standard to confirm on the specific product, not a visible or assumed feature.

## Naming Convention

When identifying spring nuts for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
(CHANNEL) SPRING (THREAD) (MATERIAL) (FINISH) Spring-Nut
```

### Example Names

- `Regular-Spring 1-5/8" 1/4"-20-Thread Electro-Galvanized Spring-Nut`
- `Short-Spring 3/8"-16-Thread Electro-Galvanized Spring-Nut`
- `Long-Spring 1/2"-13-Thread Electro-Galvanized Spring-Nut`
- `No-Spring 3/8"-16-Thread Stainless-Steel Spring-Nut`
- `Regular-Spring 3/8"-16-Thread Hot-Dip-Galvanized Spring-Nut`
- `Regular-Spring 3/16" 3/8"-16-Thread Stainless-Steel Spring-Nut`

### Convention Notes

| Descriptor   | Explanation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `(CHANNEL)`  | The optional strut channel width/profile the nut is sized for (e.g., standard 1-5/8" channel, or a narrower profile such as 13/16"). Leads the name, since fit against the specific channel's interior width and lip depth is the first compatibility check. |
| `SPRING`     | Short-Spring, Regular-Spring, Long-Spring, or No-Spring. This is the most common ordering mistake, so confirm channel depth before defaulting to Regular-Spring.                                                                                             |
| `(THREAD)`   | The thread size and pitch, matched exactly to the bolt or threaded rod (e.g., 1/4"-20, 3/8"-16, 1/2"-13). If left off, assume 1/4"-20 thread size & pitch.                                                                                                   |
| `(MATERIAL)` | Optional specifier for material such as Aluminum, Steel, Stainless-Steel, etc.                                                                                                                                                                               |
| `(FINISH)`   | Electro-Galvanized (standard interior/dry), Hot-Dip-Galvanized (exterior), Stainless-Steel (wet/sanitary/coastal, specify 304 or 316 grade separately if needed).                                                                                            |

### Ordering Help

- Always specify **channel profile, thread size, spring length, and finish** together — spring length is called out repeatedly as the single most common ordering mistake in this category, since it depends on channel depth rather than anything printed on the bolt or fitting being installed.
- Confirm the **exact channel series** (not just "1-5/8-inch strut") against the manufacturer's compatibility chart before ordering, since width, depth, slot type, and steel gauge vary enough between brands and series to cause a nut to spin, bind, or fail to seat properly.
- Match **finish to environment** — electro-galvanized for standard interior use, hot-dip galvanized or stainless steel for exterior, washdown, or corrosive settings — and confirm 304 vs. 316 stainless where chloride exposure (coastal air, de-icing salts) is a factor.
- If matching an **existing installation** or another brand's part number, use a strut brand cross-reference chart rather than assuming dimensional interchangeability from the name alone.
- Remember that the **spring itself is a holding aid, not a structural element** — a No-Spring nut reaches the same final strength once properly tightened, so it's a valid lower-cost option wherever hand-holding the nut during installation isn't a practical concern.
