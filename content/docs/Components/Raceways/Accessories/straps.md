## Overview

Straps are the fasteners used to secure conduit and raceway to a building structure — walls, ceilings, studs, beams, or other mounting surfaces — at the intervals required by code. They are distinct from hangers and clamps used for suspended or trapeze-style support; straps mount the raceway directly against or close to a flat or framed surface.

![](/images/strap-example.png)

Straps are selected based on:

1. **Hole pattern:**
    - **One-hole strap** — a single mounting hole, typically used with smaller trade sizes and lighter-duty runs; installs quickly but offers less resistance to rotation than a two-hole strap.
    - **Two-hole strap** — two mounting holes straddling the conduit, providing a more rigid, rotation-resistant mount; standard for larger trade sizes and most rigid/IMC installations.
    - **Snap-on strap** — a spring-tempered one-hole variant that snaps directly over the conduit without needing to slide the strap on from the end, speeding up installation on long runs.
2. **Clamp-back / spacer style** — a strap variant with a raised, standoff back that holds the conduit slightly off the mounting surface. This creates an air gap for corrosion resistance (especially outdoors or in damp locations) and simplifies painting or maintenance behind the raceway.
3. **Material:**
    - **Stamped steel** — the most common and economical option, typically used for smaller trade sizes (1/2" to 1-1/2").
    - **Malleable iron** — used for larger trade sizes (2" and up) and heavier-duty applications, offering greater strength than stamped steel.
    - **Stainless steel** — used in corrosive, washdown, or outdoor coastal environments where standard finishes would degrade.
    - **PVC/plastic** — used exclusively with PVC conduit runs to avoid galvanic or material incompatibility issues, and in corrosive-environment installations.
4. **Raceway compatibility** — straps are typically listed/sized for a specific raceway type or types (EMT, Rigid/IMC, or PVC); a strap sized for EMT's actual OD will not properly fit Rigid/IMC conduit of the same trade size, since the two have different outside diameters at a given trade size.

## Naming Convention

When identifying straps for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
SIZE TYPE STYLE (MATERIAL) (FINISH) Strap
```

| Descriptor   | Explanation                                                       |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `SIZE`       | Trade size diameter of the strap in inches (1/2", 2-3/4", etc.)   |
| `TYPE`       | Conduit type the strap will be attaching to (EMT, RMC, IMC, etc.) |
| `STYLE`      | Style of strap; typical styles include 1-Hole, 2-Hole, Strut,     |
| `(MATERIAL)` | Optional material specifier (Steel, Aluminum, Brass, etc).        |
| `(FINISH)`   | Optional finish specifier, for example, Electro-Galvanized.       |

### Example Names

- `1/2" EMT One-Hole Steel Strap`
- `2-1/2" EMT Two-Hole Aluminum Strap`
- `4" RMC Strut Strap`
- `3" IMC Strut Stainless Steel Strap`
- `3/4" PVC One-Hole PVC Strap`

### Convention Notes

- Lead with **SIZE** as the nominal trade size matching the conduit it will secure (e.g., 1/2", 3/4", 1", 1-1/4", 1-1/2", 2", and larger).
- State **TYPE** as the raceway type the strap is sized/listed for: EMT, RigidIMC, or PVC — never assume interchangeability, since EMT and Rigid/IMC have different actual outside diameters at the same trade size.
- State **STYLE** as the style of strap, where one-holes include a hole on one side of the strap, two-hole for holes on both sides of the strap, strut straps which include geometry that fit precisely within mounting strut, or some other style not mentioned.
- State optional **MATERIAL** as Steel, Malleable-Iron, Stainless-Steel, PVC, or some other material.
- State optional **FINISH** as an optional finishing on the material such as Electro-Galvanized, Powder-Coated (COLOR), or some other finish not mentioned.

## Typical Units of Measure

|Unit|Typical Use|
|---|---|
|**Each (EA)**|Standard unit for pricing and small quantity orders, especially larger trade sizes (2" and up).|
|**Box**|Common bulk packaging for smaller trade sizes (1/2" through 1-1/2"), typically packed **25 or 100 per box**.|
|**Per 100 (C)**|Common pricing/weight basis in manufacturer catalogs (e.g., "lbs. per 100"), especially for stamped steel one-hole straps ordered in bulk.|
|**Master Case/Carton**|Distributor bulk quantity, often several boxes per master case; used for large project takeoffs.|

### Ordering Help

- Always specify **trade size, raceway type, hole count, and material** together — a strap sized for EMT will not properly fit Rigid/IMC conduit of the same nominal trade size, since actual outside diameters differ between raceway types.
- Confirm whether **clamp-back/spacer construction** is needed for the installation environment (exterior, damp, or washdown locations) before ordering standard straps, since retrofitting standoff spacing after installation is impractical.
- For **corrosive or coastal environments**, confirm stainless steel or PVC material rather than standard zinc-plated steel, and verify compatibility with any dissimilar-metal fasteners being used.
- Plan strap quantities against the **NEC's maximum support spacing** for the specific raceway type and trade size being installed — under-ordering support hardware is a common source of change orders and failed inspections.
