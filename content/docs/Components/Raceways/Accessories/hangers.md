## Overview

A conduit hanger suspends a conduit run from an overhead structural point — typically a length of threaded rod dropped from the ceiling or structure above — rather than fastening it directly to a flat wall surface the way a strap does. This is the field-standard method for supporting conduit runs across open ceiling spaces, mechanical rooms, and any overhead routing where there's no adjacent wall or strut framework to strap to.

![](/images/conduit-hanger-example.png)

The most common type is universally known in the field by its nickname, **"minnie"** (also spelled "minny," and originally derived from the Minerallac brand name, though the term is now used generically across manufacturers such as Steel City, Caddy/Erico, and Bridgeport). The steel conduit hanger secures 1/2" to 4" rigid (galvanized) or EMT conduit to a support using the hole on top of the hanger — typically suspended from strut or directly from concrete using a piece of threaded rod, though it can also attach conduit directly to an insulator or other surface. A minnie is a generally U-shaped device formed of sheet metal, with a flat base and two arms that have a cylindrically rounded part shaped to fit against the conduit; the outer ends of the arms have aligned holes to permit a bolt to pass through, clamping the conduit tightly when tightened. It is available with or without the lower bolt and nut included, so confirm whether hardware is bundled before ordering.

**Field installation practice worth noting:** multiple minnies can be stacked on a single piece of threaded rod to support several individual conduit runs at different heights from one anchor point — a common time-saving technique that avoids setting a separate structural anchor for every conduit in a group.

A related but distinct product is the **clevis hanger**, consisting of a lower U-shaped hanger member that supports the conduit/pipe and an upper hanger member that connects the assembly to the support rod, with the two members joined by a fastener through aligned openings in their arms. Clevis hangers are the standard product in mechanical/plumbing pipe support but see far more limited and debated use for electrical conduit: field practitioners have noted that a clevis hanger may not "securely fasten" the conduit as required by the NEC's raceway-securing sections, since the lower member is not rigidly locked to the conduit the way a minnie's clamped arms are. For standard EMT and rigid conduit support, the minnie remains the default field choice; a clevis hanger is more appropriate for larger pipe or where the manufacturer's specific listing supports the application.

**Selection factors:**

1. **Conduit trade size range** — a single minnie size typically accommodates one specific trade size (e.g., 1/2" through 4" EMT/rigid, sized individually per hanger).
2. **Hardware inclusion** — hangers are sold both with and without the lower clamping bolt/nut; confirm which is being quoted, since this affects whether additional hardware needs to be ordered separately.
3. **Rod/mounting hole size** — the top mounting hole must match the threaded rod size being used to suspend the hanger (commonly 1/4" or 3/8" rod for typical conduit runs).
4. **Material and finish** — standard electro-galvanized steel is the default; stainless steel is specified for corrosive or washdown environments.

## Naming Convention

When identifying conduit hangers for vendor ordering, use the following naming structure, listing attributes in this order:

```
SIZE (HARDWARE) (MATERIAL) (ROD) Conduit-Hanger
```

### Example Names

- `3/4" With-Bolt 3/8in Galvanized-Steel Conduit-Hanger`
- `1" Without-Bolt 3/8in Galvanized-Steel Conduit-Hanger`
- `2" With-Bolt 1/4in Galvanized-Steel Conduit-Hanger`
- `4" With-Bolt 3/8in Stainless-Steel Conduit-Hanger`
- `1/2" With-Bolt 1/4in Galvanized-Steel Conduit-Hanger`
- `1-1/2" Steel Hole-1" Conduit-Hanger`

### Convention Notes

| Descriptor       | Explanation                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `SIZE`           | The conduit trade size the hanger is built to accommodate (e.g., 1/2", 1", 1-1/2", up to 4"). Leads the name, since fit is the first compatibility check.                                                                     |
| `(HARDWARE)`     | With-Bolt or Without-Bolt, indicating whether the lower clamping bolt and nut are included with the hanger. Confirm this explicitly, since it's a common point of confusion on quotes. Although, assume default of With-Bolt. |
| `(MATERIAL)`     | Galvanized-Steel (standard) or Stainless-Steel (corrosive/washdown environments), or regular Steel; assume regular Steel as the default.                                                                                      |
| `(ROD)`          | The top mounting hole size, matched to the threaded rod being used (commonly 1/4" or 3/8"); assume 1/4" as the default.                                                                                                       |
| `Conduit-Hanger` | Most foremen and field personnel call these parts "Minnies" or "Minis" for short; very common alias.                                                                                                                          |

### Ordering Help

- Always specify **conduit size, hardware inclusion, rod size, and material** together — a "minnie" order without these fields risks arriving without the clamping bolt or with a mounting hole that doesn't match the rod already on hand.
- Confirm **threaded rod is ordered as its own line item** if not already on hand — a hanger and its rod are typically sold separately, and rod size must match the hanger's top mounting hole.
- For runs supporting **multiple conduits from a single anchor point**, plan hanger quantity to match the intended stacking arrangement on the rod rather than assuming one hanger per anchor.
- Confirm the installation still satisfies the applicable **support spacing interval** for the raceway type and trade size being run — stacking multiple hangers on one rod saves anchoring time but does not change how frequently the run itself needs to be supported along its length.
- If a **clevis hanger** is being considered as a substitute for a standard minnie on an electrical conduit run, verify it is acceptable for that raceway type and application before ordering, since its securing method differs materially from a minnie's clamped-arm design.
